Components

Alert

An alert is a message that lives in the page: a warning above a form, a failed sync above a list, a tip under a heading. It stays until the problem is fixed or the person dismisses it. It is a card with a tone and one row of parts, not a second kind of surface.

When to use

Do

Say what happened and what to do about it, in that order, and put the fix in a button: Card declined, then Update card. Put the alert next to the thing it is about.

Don't

Use an alert for a passing confirmation (use a toast), for a decision (use a dialog), or stack several on one screen. With more than one, none of them is read.

Examples

Status

Four statuses, each with its own icon shape and a title that says what happened, so colour is never the only cue. The hue comes from the tone system and keeps 7:1 text in every palette; in wire all four go grey and still read.

Information: Sync runs at night

New cards upload over Wi-⁠Fi between 1 and 5 am. Nothing to do.

Success: Deck imported

214 cards are ready in Shapes. None were duplicates.

Warning: Storage almost full

You have used 92% of your space. New photos may not back up.

Error: Card declined

Your bank refused the payment. Nothing was charged.

With an action

One action that fixes the problem, and at most one quiet second. Buttons set data-variant to tone and carry the alert's own data-tone, so they take its colours and stay readable by construction. The primary one fills on hover like every button.

Warning: Storage almost full

You have used 92% of your space.

Error: Card declined

Your bank refused the payment. Nothing was charged.

Dismissible

Add data-dismissible to the alert and data-sg-dismiss to a real button. SG.dismiss hides it and, if focus was inside, moves focus to the next control instead of dropping it on the page. Try it with the keyboard.

Information: New: shared decks

Invite someone to a deck and you both see progress as it happens.

Tip

Press and hold a card to drag it to another deck.

Banner row

data-size="sm" puts icon, text, action and close on one row when the card has room (about 26rem, a tablet or a phone turned sideways), for things that are state rather than news: offline, read-only, trial ending. Narrower, as on a phone held upright, the action drops under the words and lines up with them, not with the icon. Keep it to one or two lines. It stays a card: the frame and the tone are the same.

Warning: You are offline. Changes sync when you reconnect.

Information: Your trial ends in 3 days.

Success: Backup finished. 214 cards are safe.

Bold emphasis

For the one message that must not be missed. data-emphasis set to bold flips the tone to a saturated fill with near-white text. Use it once per screen, at most. It goes on the same element as data-tone.

Error: Payment failed

Update your card by Friday to keep your plan.

Announcing a new alert

These two buttons add an alert while you are on the page. Save draft puts a success alert into a role="status" container (polite). Sync now pretends the sync failed and puts an error into a role="alert" one (assertive, it interrupts). Both containers exist from the start and are empty; the alert is inserted into them.

In context

The alert sits directly above the thing it is about, with its fix inside it. The list below it stays readable, and says which decks are waiting.

Which ARIA role?

The role decides whether a screen reader says the alert out loud. Most alerts need none.

Choosing a live-region role for an alert
The alert...UseWhy
is on the page when it loads (a trial banner, a permanent tip)No roleIt is ordinary content. Announcing it on every visit is noise, and a screen reader finds it by reading the page.
appears or changes later and is useful but not urgent (saved, synced, offline)role="status"Polite: waits for the current sentence to finish. Equivalent to aria-live="polite".
appears later and needs attention now (a payment failed, the session is ending)role="alert"Assertive: it interrupts. Use sparingly; every alert trains people to ignore the next.
describes a field (a hint or an error under an input)Not an alertUse aria-describedby on the field, so the message is read when the field is focused.
  • Put the role on a container that already exists and is empty, then insert the alert into it. A live region created together with its text is announced by some screen readers and skipped by others.
  • Do not add aria-live to a role="status" or role="alert" element: the role already implies it.
  • Do not use role="alert" for a dismissible tip. Dismissing is not an emergency.

Anatomy

  1. Card (.card.alert): the 2px frame, the tone fill and the radius role are the card's. The frame is a real border, so forced colours still draws it. The alert adds no shadow: it is not pressable.
  2. Icon: a 24px mask, decorative. A different shape per status: information circle, check circle, warning triangle, alert circle.
  3. Title (.alert__title, optional): the headline in bold, with a visually hidden status word in front (Warning: ).
  4. Text (.alert__text): what happened and what to do. 16px, in the card's full ink (at least 7:1), never the softer one.
  5. Actions (.alert__actions, optional): .btn with data-variant set to tone. They sit under the text, or at the end of a banner row.
  6. Dismiss (.alert__close, optional): a small circle button with a 44×44px hit area, named for what it closes.

API

Alert classes and attributes
AttributeValuesEffect
class="card alert"The container. Padded part: .card__body. Parts: alert__main, alert__title, alert__text, alert__actions, alert__close.
data-toneok, warn, bad, info; 1 to 6, inkThe status or the rhythm. No attribute = a plain paper card.
data-emphasisboldSaturated fill, near-white text. On the same element as data-tone.
data-sizesmBanner row with tighter padding.
data-dismissible + data-sg-dismissClose button handled by SG.dismiss. data-sg-dismiss="#id" targets another element. A cancelable sg:dismiss event fires first.

Accessibility

Alert keyboard and screen reader behaviour
InteractionBehaviour
Screen readerNo role: read in page order like any paragraph. status: spoken politely when it appears. alert: spoken at once. The hidden prefix ("Warning: ") gives the severity without relying on the icon.
TabActions, then the dismiss button, in reading order. The alert itself is not focusable.
Enter / Space on dismissHides the alert and moves focus to the next focusable element, so keyboard users keep their place (WCAG 2.4.3).
TouchButtons and the dismiss circle have a 44×44px hit area with 8px between.
ColourNever the only cue (WCAG 1.4.1): icon shape plus a title. The wire palette removes hue entirely and the four statuses remain distinguishable.
Zoom / text sizeThe words grow with the reader's text size, and so does the status icon, which leads the title's first line (1.25 times its size, never under 24px: 40px at 200%); the insets and the 36px dismiss circle stop at their 100% size, so the words keep the room. A title that wraps is balanced, so its last word is never alone on a line. Text wraps between words; the row reflows at 320px and at 200% text. In a narrow alert the icon and the dismiss circle share a row above the words; a banner row keeps its icon beside the first line. An action label that is wider than the whole alert wraps inside its pill, which keeps its round ends.
Forced coloursThe card's border becomes CanvasText; each button keeps its own border.

Tokens used

Everything comes through Card and Button: --tone-bg, --tone-ink, --paper, --ink, --bw, --radius-card. The alert itself adds --type-body, --text-md, --lh-body, --lh-snug, --space-1, and the chrome caps --chrome-1, --chrome-2, --chrome-3, --chrome-ic, --chrome-ctl-sm.
