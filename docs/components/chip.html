Components

Chip

A chip is a small control you operate: switch a filter on, pick one of a few choices, or remove something you added. It is a pill you can press.

When to use

Do

Use chips for compact, optional choices next to content: filter by category, pick a size, who this goes to. One or two words per label.

Don't

Use a chip for something you cannot press: a status or count is a tag, not a chip. Don't use chips to move between screens (that is the dock), to swap panels (tabs) or to choose one of two or three values (a segmented control).

Five kinds, five jobs

The kinds of chip and their semantics
KindUse it to…Built as
FilterTurn a filter on or off. Any number can be on.<button aria-pressed>, or checkboxes
ChoicePick exactly one of a small set.Native radio inputs in a group
InputShow something the user added, and let them remove it.A flat chip with its own <button> named "Remove…"
MoreReveal the chips that did not fit ("3 more").<button aria-expanded>
ActionDo one thing: "Add tag", "Try an example".<button> or <a>

Toggle and choice are different questions. A toggle chip says this filter is on (aria-pressed); a choice chip says this is the value (a radio, so arrow keys move and choose, and the value posts with a form).

Examples

Filter chips

Each is a toggle button. On, a chip is filled, its frame doubles (a 2px ring in the surface colour just inside the border, so it keeps the size of the chips beside it), and a check takes the place of its icon, so it is never told apart by colour alone. The fill means on and nothing else: a pointer over a chip raises it, a press tints it, but only an on chip is solid. The check is drawn by the chip itself; you only write the label. data-sg-chips on the row turns on the toggling, and your app listens for sg:chip-toggle.

Choice chips

One choice from a few. These are real radio buttons dressed as chips: Left and Right move and choose, the group is one tab stop, and there is no script.

Card size

Multi-select with checkboxes

Filters that belong in a form can be checkboxes: the same markup with type="checkbox". Use buttons when the chips act on the page at once, checkboxes when they are submitted.

Remind me by

Input chips

The chip itself is text and flat: it holds a value, it does not invite a press. Only its remove button acts, and it has its own name, Remove Anna Kim. Removing a chip moves focus to the next chip's remove button (else the previous, else an element you name) and announces "Anna Kim removed. 2 left." Delete and Backspace also work on the focused button. Here an Undo button appears once someone is removed and puts the last one back, with focus on their chip; it hides again when there is nothing left to undo.

"N more"

When there are more chips than you want to show, end the visible ones with a dashed more chip. It is a disclosure button: aria-expanded says whether the rest are showing and aria-controls names them. A plus in front of the label becomes a minus when open. The revealed chips come after the button in the DOM, so a keyboard user tabs straight into them.

Action chips and link chips

Without aria-pressed a chip is just a pill-shaped button. Make it an <a> when it goes somewhere.

About spacing

One scrolling line

A wrapping row is the safest default, because nothing is hidden. When vertical space is tight, add the scroller class: the row scrolls sideways, and the chip cut off at the edge says there is more. Let the row run to the edge of the screen (bleed inside an .app column; this stage does the same), so the cut is made by the frame and reads as "more this way", not as a pill sliced inside the layout. Where the cut falls is not left to luck: at rest the chips' side padding changes a little, all by the same amount, until the chip at the edge shows about half of itself (never a sliver, never a chip missing only its round end). Tab through it and each focused chip scrolls fully into view. Name the row so it can be found.

Sizes and tone

Medium is drawn at 36px, large at 44px. Both have a 44px hit area, so two wrapped rows never overlap their targets. data-variant="tone" takes the fill from data-tone on the chip itself, as a button does: the tone's pale colour at rest, its solid colour when on. Use one size in a row.

States

The look follows ARIA and native attributes. The solid fill is kept for on: hover and keyboard focus raise a chip with its hard shadow, a press sinks it with a light tint, and only an on chip is filled (with its doubled frame and its check). The is-* classes only force a state for this page.

Anatomy

  1. Frame: a pill with a 2px --line border, 36px tall (44px with data-size="lg"), 16px of side padding (24px when large). The border is always there, so forced-colours mode still draws an edge.
  2. Leading icon (optional, chip__icon): 1.15em of the label (16px at normal text, growing with the words), decorative, beside the first line of the label. It gives its place to the check when the chip is on, so the chip does not change width.
  3. Check: a mask the chip draws in front of the label when on, the size of the icon it replaces. With the fill and the doubled frame it is the third cue; on a tone fill, where a second ring read as a heavy black frame, the check is the cue. A "more" chip shows a plus, or a minus when open.
  4. Doubled frame (on): a 2px ring in the surface colour inside the border, between the frame and the fill. Drawn inside, so an on chip is exactly as big as its neighbours.
  5. Label: 14px semibold, sentence case. It never changes when the chip is pressed.
  6. Fill: solid only when the chip is on. A press shows as a light tint.
  7. Hard shadow: only when it can be pressed (hover, focus). An input chip is flat.
  8. Remove button (chip__remove, input chips only): a 24px circle with a 20px glyph inside the chip, at every text size (it is its own control), with its own 44px target, named with what it removes.
  9. Hit area: an invisible ::after that guarantees 44×44px.

Accessibility

Chip keyboard and screen reader behaviour
InteractionBehaviour
Tab / Shift+TabEvery filter and action chip is its own tab stop. A radio group of choice chips is one tab stop. An input chip's only tab stop is its remove button.
Enter / SpaceToggles a filter chip, presses an action chip, opens the disclosure, removes an input chip (on its remove button). Space toggles a checkbox chip.
Left / RightIn a choice-chip group: move and choose (native radio behaviour). Disabled ones are skipped.
Delete / BackspaceOn a focused remove button: removes the chip.
Screen reader"Food and drink, toggle button, pressed". Choice: "Medium, radio button, 2 of 4". Remove: "Remove Anna Kim, button". Disclosure: "4 more, button, collapsed". After a removal: "Anna Kim removed. 2 left."
Touch44×44px targets, at least 8px between chips. A scrolling row can be swiped, and every chip is also reachable with Tab.
Zoom / text sizeThe label grows with the text, and so do the leading icon and the check, which sit in its line; the side padding, the gap, the 44px target and an input chip's remove button stop at their 100% size, so at 200% text a label keeps the room and holds one line wherever the row allows. A label too long for the row wraps at its spaces, beside its icon, and the corner stays the one-line radius, so a wrapped chip is a soft rectangle and never an oval. A word never breaks unless it alone is wider than the row. A wrapping row never scrolls sideways; in a scrolling row a chip keeps its whole label (it does not shrink).
Reduced motionNo lift travel; the fill and frame change stay.
Forced coloursEvery chip keeps its border; raised and focused chips get a 4px Highlight outline; an on chip gets a 4px frame and keeps its check.
  • The label never changes when a chip is pressed: state is carried by aria-pressed (APG toggle button), not by rewording.
  • Name every row (role="group" with aria-label, or a fieldset with a legend) so a screen-reader user knows what the chips are for.
  • When a removable chip disappears, focus must go somewhere sensible, or keyboard users fall back to the top of the page. The script handles it; if you remove chips yourself, do the same.
  • A choice chip is a radio: do not add aria-pressed to it.
