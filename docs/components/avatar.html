Components

Avatar

A person, a team or an app, as a circle. A photo when there is one, their initials when there is not. In this guide circles are the shape of things that act, so an avatar stays a plain circle until it is a link or a button; then it raises like every other pressable circle.

When to use

Do

Name it. If the person's name is written beside the avatar, hide the avatar (aria-hidden). If not, it is an image with their name as its label.

Don't

Show presence with a coloured dot and nothing else, or make each circle in an overlapping stack its own button. Overlap and small targets do not mix.

Photo or initials

The initials are the avatar; the photo is an <img alt=""> laid over them. If the photo fails to load, a few lines of script (src/js/30-avatar.js) hide it and the initials show. The name lives on the avatar, so nothing about what a screen reader says depends on the photo.

With no photo, data-tone picks the field behind the initials. Choose it from the person's id (not their name, and not at random) so they keep the same colour everywhere. It is identity, never meaning.

When the name is already on the page

Next to a visible name the avatar is decoration. Hide it, or the name is read twice. A List row is the usual place: the avatar keeps the lead slot and the name stays beside it at any text size.

Sizes

Five steps: 28, 36, 44 (the default), 64 and 96px. An avatar is a picture, not a line of text, so it keeps its size when the reader's text grows (like an icon in a tab bar), and the initials are sized from the circle: they keep their proportion, a name beside the avatar keeps its room, and a stack stays one row. At 28px the two letters are set a little narrower, so they clear the frame.

Presence

A dot on the circle's corner says whether someone is around. Each state has its own shape, so it reads without colour: a filled circle is online, a filled diamond is away, a filled square is busy and an empty ring is offline. The state also goes into the avatar's name, because a dot has no text of its own. Presence starts at the 36px size; a 28px circle cannot carry four legible shapes.

Pressable

An avatar that goes somewhere (your profile) or does something (switch account) is an <a> or a <button> with the same class. It then behaves like every pressable circle: it raises with a hard shadow when it can be pressed, takes a light tint while it is pressed, and has a 44×44px hit area even at 28px. It is never filled with a solid colour, because that would wipe out the person's colour or photo. It needs an aria-label that says what pressing does, not just who it shows.

MO

Stack

Who is in this? A flat row of overlapping circles, the last one on top, in DOM order. The closing +3 is inverted (ink fill) so it cannot be mistaken for a person. It is a list, so a screen reader says how many people there are; give the list a label.

Sizes are set once, on the stack: data-size on the list. Overlap is 22% of the diameter, so two letters stay legible from 44px up; at 36px and below use photos or a single letter, as in the first row below. A stack is one row at every text size: its circles keep their size, so keep it to three or four faces and a count.

A stack you can open

Overlapping circles cannot each be a 44px target, so the stack itself is not interactive. If people should be able to see the full list, make the overflow a button. It keeps its 44px hit area, and the people in the stack stay text.

Anatomy

  1. Circle: --radius-pill with a 2px frame (--line). The frame is real, so forced-colours mode still draws it.
  2. Initials: --type-ctl, semi-expanded (semi-condensed at 28px), sized from the circle: about 36% of its diameter. On --paper-2 or the tone.
  3. Photo (optional): <img alt="">, object-fit: cover, laid over the initials. No background of its own.
  4. Presence dot (optional): a 2px-framed shape on the lower corner, with a paper halo. Shape carries the state; colour only backs it up.
  5. Hit area (pressable only): an invisible ::after that guarantees 44×44px.
  6. Hard shadow (pressable only): 4px, zero blur, when it is raised (hover, focus). It means "you can press this".

API

Avatar and avatar stack attributes
AttributeValuesEffect
class="avatar"The circle. A <span> when it only shows someone; an <a> or <button> when it acts.
data-sizexs 28, sm 36, lg 64, xl 96 (default 44)On an avatar or on its stack. Pixels at any text size (the initials follow the circle).
data-tone1…6, inkThe field behind the initials. Identity colour; never meaning.
data-presenceonline, away, busy, offlineThe dot and its shape. Also say it in the aria-label.
data-overflowThe "+3" that closes a stack: ink fill, paper text.
aria-current="true" / aria-pressed="true"Pressable avatars: the frame doubles; the colour or photo stays.
aria-disabled="true"Pressable avatars: dashed frame, faint ink.
class="avatar-stack"A <ul role="list"> of <li> avatars, overlapping by 22% of the diameter, in one row. Needs an aria-label.

Accessibility

Avatar keyboard and screen reader behaviour
InteractionBehaviour
TabA static avatar is not a tab stop. A link or button avatar is, with the standard ring.
Enter / SpaceActivate a button avatar (Enter follows a link). Native elements give this for free.
Screen reader"Maya Okafor, image", or "Your profile, link". The presence state is in the name. A stack reads as "Shared with Maya, Jonas, Priya and 3 more, list, 4 items".
ColourPresence is a shape (circle, diamond, square, ring) and a word in the name. The field colour is identity, not meaning.
TouchPressable avatars keep a 44×44px hit area at every size. Stack items are not pressable; the overflow button is.
Zoom / text sizeAn avatar is a picture: at 200% text it keeps its size and its initials (browser zoom still scales it), so the name beside it keeps its room and a stack stays one row. The photo is cropped, never stretched.
Reduced motionNo travel when it raises or sinks; the shadow and the pressed tint still show.
Forced coloursThe frame becomes CanvasText; presence shapes repaint in system colours (offline stays empty); pressable avatars get a 4px Highlight outline.
