Components

Accordion

Sections that fold away, so a long page stays short until you ask for more. It is the browser's own <details> and <summary>, drawn in the Card panel's language: a 2px frame, and an ink bar with a square control that shows + or −. No script.

When to use

Do

Use it for content people may not need: answers, advanced settings, the details behind a number. Make each heading say what is inside, in a few words.

Don't

Hide what most people need to finish the task, nest one accordion in another, or use it to switch between views. That is a set of tabs.

One open at a time

Give the items the same name and the browser keeps one open: opening another closes the first. No JavaScript is involved. Where name is not supported, items simply open independently, which is still correct.

How does a streak work?

Study at least one card before midnight and your streak goes up by one. Miss a day and it starts again at zero, unless you still have a freeze.

Can I study without a connection?

Yes. Decks you have opened are saved on your device. Your progress is sent when you are back online.

What happens to cards I get wrong?

They come back sooner: tomorrow, then in three days, then weekly until you know them.

Can I share a deck?

Send a link. People you invite study their own copy, and you can see who is studying yours.

Many open

Leave name off and each item opens on its own, for settings or filters where people compare sections. The open items keep their bar, so you can see at a glance which are open without reading a thing.

Reminders

Remind me at 18:00 on days I have not studied. Quiet hours are 22:00 to 07:00.

Sync

Decks sync on Wi-Fi only. The last sync was yesterday at 21:40.

Privacy

Only people you invite can see your decks. Nobody sees your streak unless you share it.

Panels

With data-variant set to panels each item is a Card panel of its own: framed, and the bar is always there, closed or open. Use it when the sections are the content (a dashboard of groups), not a list of questions. data-tone on an item tints its body; the ink tone flips the bar to paper, as on the card.

States

A head is a pressable thing, so its states only set --fill: a tint of the head's own text colour laid over its fill. It never lifts, because it lives inside a frame. open is the native attribute; the control and the bar both follow it. The is-* classes below only force a state for this page.

Anatomy

  1. Frame: .accordion, 2px --line, --radius-card. Items are separated by 1px rules. In panels each item has its own frame.
  2. Head: the <summary>. At least 56px tall, the whole row is the target, inset 16px at the sides at any text size. Closed it is paper; open it is the bar (--ink fill, --paper text), as on the card.
  3. Control: a 20px square with a 2px frame (--radius-ctl) holding a 12px + or −, drawn by ::before and ::after, so there is nothing to forget in the markup. It keeps that size at any text size.
  4. Title: --type-ctl, wraps; write the heading, not a sentence.
  5. Body: --type-body with 16px padding, at most --measure wide. data-flush removes the padding for a list or a grid; a List's rows are inset 16px too, so their text lines up with the heads.

API

Accordion attributes
AttributeValuesEffect
class="accordion"The group. A <div> holding the items.
data-variantpanels (default: rows)Rows: one frame, hairlines between, a bar only on open items. Panels: each item is its own framed panel with its bar always on.
class="accordion__item"A <details>. The same name on several makes them exclusive. open opens it.
data-tone1…6, inkOn an item: tints its body. Tone is rhythm, not meaning.
class="accordion__head"The <summary>. aria-disabled="true" makes it unavailable and keeps it focusable.
class="accordion__title"Wrap the heading text in it.
class="accordion__body"data-flushThe content. Flush: no padding, no measure limit.

Accessibility

Accordion keyboard and screen reader behaviour
InteractionBehaviour
TabEach head is a tab stop, in page order. Closed content is out of the tab order; open content is in it. The ring is drawn inside the head, in paper on a bar.
Arrow keysNot bound. The WAI-ARIA accordion pattern makes Up / Down / Home / End between heads optional, and native <summary> does not do it; Tab moves between heads.
Enter / SpaceToggle the focused head. Native <summary> does this. Focus stays on the head.
Screen readerThe head is a focusable disclosure control named by its text, with an expanded or collapsed state; Chromium exposes exactly that. Nothing is added in script, so the state always matches what you see. VoiceOver, TalkBack and NVDA are not verified.
ColourOpen is an inverted bar, a − sign and the native expanded state; closed is paper and a + sign. None of it is colour.
TouchThe whole head is the target, at least 56px tall. No gesture is involved.
Zoom / text sizeTitles grow and wrap between words at 200% text; the control, the 56px floor and the 16px insets keep their size, so the words keep the width.
Reduced motionOpening fades the body in and drops it 8px. Reduced motion removes the drop and keeps the fade. Closing is instant.
Forced coloursFrames become CanvasText; the open head gets a 4px line under it and the − sign; hover and focus get a 4px Highlight outline inside the head.
