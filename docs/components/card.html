Components

Card

A card holds one idea. It is a frame with slots; what goes in a slot is another element's business. This is the owner's original element, ported unchanged: same class names, same anatomy, same states.

House rules

  • Reads roles only (--paper, --ink, --tone-N). No colour literals.
  • One shape gesture per card: round it or notch it, never both.
  • A hard shadow means "you can press this". Nothing else gets one.
  • Never put cqi on .card itself. A container's own cqi resolves against its parent. Sizes that use it live on the parts.
  • A card needs a width from its parent (a grid cell, a block). container-type: inline-size gives it no intrinsic width, so it collapses inside a shrink-wrapping flex row.
  • Do not pad .card itself. Padding goes on the parts. (The one-line layouts, row and ghost, are the exception: their whole card is one padded row.)

Nine parts, all optional except the frame. A card is whichever of them it needs.

Panel

A card with a title bar. Lists, settings, anything that folds away.

.card__bar .card__label .card__ctl .card__list

The bar is the frame made thicker, not a separate header. On the inverted tone it flips to paper, or it would vanish into the card. The control is a disclosure: it keeps one name (the panel's title) and aria-expanded carries the state, so a screen reader hears "Decks, button, expanded". data-sg-toggle (from styleguide.js) shows and hides what aria-controls points to. Each list row is one target: the link is stretched over the whole row, and the row inverts on hover, focus and press.

States

Two registered numbers drive the interaction: --lift (0 flat, 1 raised) and --fill (0 outline, 1 filled). Every state just sets them, so hover, focus and pressed share one definition and a single transition animates both. Keyboard and pointer get the same lift.

Selecting a card

"Selected" is not one attribute. Use the one that is valid for what the card is; aria-selected is not allowed on an article or a link.

How to mark a card as selected
The card is…MarkupAnnounced as
a link to the current page or steparia-current="page" (or "step")"current page"
a toggle button<button class="card" aria-pressed="true">"toggle button, pressed"
an option in a listbox or a tabrole="option" or "tab" with aria-selected="true""selected"
a choice in a radio or checkbox groupa real <input> inside; the card reads the input's state"radio button, checked"

The look is the same for all of them: a ring that doubles the frame, so nothing shifts. On the inverted tone the ring gets a gap of canvas, or it would be ink on ink.

Real copy

A card is a frame that clips, so anything that cannot wrap would be cut off silently. Every text slot wraps between words (overflow-wrap: break-word: a word breaks only when it alone is wider than the card, and titles hyphenate where the browser has a dictionary), rows and feet wrap, a figure stays on one line and shrinks to fit, and a portrait's word shrinks to fit its card. The accessibility gate renders these specimens at 200% text to prove it. Test a new variant with the longest real value each slot will ever hold, and give the grid a minimum wide enough that it falls to one column before a title would have to break inside a word (13rem here).

Accessibility

  • One tab stop per link card; its accessible name is the title text.
  • Every target is at least 44px: the action circle, the bar control, list rows, the foot. Inline links in the foot are at least 24px tall.
  • The focus ring is always visible, and always against a colour it contrasts with: outside the frame on the card itself; inset on the bar and on list rows, in the bar's colours; and on any toned card in that tone's ink over a halo in its fill. The accessibility gate measures this for every focusable in every appearance.
  • Meters use role="meter" with aria-valuemin, aria-valuemax and aria-valuenow.
  • Decorative icons are hidden (aria-hidden, true); a control whose only content is an icon gets an aria-label.
  • Reset lists keep role="list" so Safari keeps the semantics that list-style: none would drop.
  • A card title is not always an h3: use the heading level that fits the page.
  • A disabled card is not an anchor: aria-disabled set to true plus the class, and remove the href.
  • Forced-colours mode is handled: shadows and fills vanish there, so hover is a 2px Highlight outline, focus a 4px one with a gap, and selected an inner frame line. The three look different and none of them moves the layout.
  • Selection uses the attribute that is valid for the role (see Selecting a card). A card with no heading gets aria-labelledby.
  • Right-to-left text is supported: the notch moves to the top-left and the stack's sheets fall to the other side.

Do and don't

Do
  • Give each card one idea and one place to go.
  • Use tones for rhythm across a grid, never to carry meaning.
  • Let the grid place cards and the card style itself.
Don't
  • Nest cards, or put two competing actions in one link card.
  • Add a shadow at rest, a stripe down one edge, or a gradient.
  • Fake "disabled" with opacity. Use the state.
  • Centre-align body text.

Known limits

  • The notch is square only. The upgrade path is corner-shape: scoop as progressive enhancement once it is supported everywhere that matters.
  • The stretched link's border band needs overflow-clip-margin. Safari ignores it, leaving a 2px band at the very edge of a link card where a click does nothing.
  • Text inside a link card cannot be selected by dragging, because the stretched link sits on top.
  • The portrait card reveals its answer; the animated flip itself is a motion design decision not made yet.
  • A link card moves 2px up and left on hover, and the pointer can sit in the 2px band it just left. Parked exactly on the bottom or right edge, the hover can flicker. Interior positions are stable.
  • In soft corners the inset focus ring on the bar control is partly clipped by the card's radius at the corner. It stays visible.
  • The stack's sheets are a shape, not an elevation: the one place a hard shadow appears on something you cannot press.
  • In forced-colours mode the notch becomes a plain rectangle, and the stack is one outline around the card instead of two sheets.
  • A card is a container, so it has no intrinsic width. It keeps a floor of min(10rem, 100%) so it never collapses to its borders in a shrink-to-fit parent (a flex row, a dialog, a table cell), but it still wants a real width from its parent.
  • The hard shadow of a lifted link card and the focus ring are kept apart (the ring sits a shadow's width further out), so keyboard focus is a clear ring and not a thick band.
  • The frame is painted over the content and the content is clipped at the frame's outer edge (overflow-clip-margin). Safari ignores that property: there the card clips at the inner edge as before, and in soft corners a faint hairline can still show where an ink bar meets the frame.
