Components

Filmstrip

A row of small pictures that chooses what the big picture shows. One choice at a time, so the thumbnails are radio buttons: the browser gives you one tab stop, arrow keys that move and choose, and a value for a form. It is the photo editor’s tray: look at the big picture, pick from the small ones.

When to use

Do

Use it when one picture is the subject and the others are alternatives to it: angles of a product, frames of a roll, backdrops behind a cut-out. The strip is a choice, so it always has exactly one thing chosen.

Don’t

Use it to browse a set where every picture matters equally (that is a Gallery), or to step through in order (a Carousel). Do not let a thumbnail do anything but choose.

Examples

Photos

Choose a thumbnail with a tap, a click, or the arrow keys. The stage is a Media figure: its picture, alt text and caption follow the choice, and the new picture gets the loading state if it is slow. The thumbnail’s own alt names the choice; data-alt on the radio describes the picture for the stage.

Choose a photo

Swatches

A thumbnail does not have to be a photo. Here the stage is a cut-out on a --paper-2 mat, and the strip picks the tone behind it. data-shape="circle" makes the thumbnails round, because choosing is acting. The first choice, None, is always offered. A swatch has no picture to name it, so it carries a visually hidden name.

Choose a backdrop

Tones carry rhythm, never meaning. The palette decides each slot’s hue, so name the choices for what they are to the person (“Gift card”, the backdrop kept for gift-card photos), not for their colour.

States

Each thumbnail is a pressable thing, so it lifts on hover and keyboard focus (the hard shadow, no fill), and a press puts it back on the strip and tints it at once, so a tap on a touch screen shows before the stage changes. The chosen one doubles its frame and wears a check badge: two cues that survive a pale photo, the wire palette and forced colours, and the picture itself is never painted over. Unavailable is dashed, with disabled on the radio.

Sizes

data-size is sm (56px), the default 72px, or lg (96px). They keep that size at 200% text (a thumbnail is the frame of a picture, not a word), so the strip shows as many choices as before. The hit area is the whole thumbnail, so even the small one is over 44px.

When there are more thumbnails than room, the strip scrolls sideways and says so: the first thumbnail past the edge is always cut part way (40 to 70% of it shows), never a whole one ending neatly at the edge, which would look like the end of the strip. The script gets there by setting the gap (from 8px), and only when no gap can do it by changing the thumbnails’ size, by an eighth at most. After every choice, and on load, the strip brings the chosen thumbnail into view with a neighbour cut on each side that has more to show.

In context

The photo editor’s backdrop tool: its name, the stage, the choices under it, and cancel / apply in the same two corners they are in every tool. The circles act; the stage holds. The name sits above the stage, not between the two circles, so at any text size the circles keep their corners.

Anatomy

  1. Frame .filmstrip: one 2px frame around the stage and the strip, --radius-card, clipped.
  2. Stage: a .media figure with an id. Its picture, alt, tone and caption text follow the choice.
  3. Strip .filmstrip__strip: a <fieldset> with a (visually hidden) <legend> naming the choice, and aria-controls pointing at the stage. Closed off by the 2px rule.
  4. Track .filmstrip__track: the sideways scroller. Its padding is the room for the focus ring and the doubled frame, which must not clip.
  5. Thumbnail .filmstrip__thumb: a <label> around a real <input type="radio"> (stretched over it, invisible: it is the hit area and the focus target) and an img, a swatch tone, or an icon.
  6. Check badge: a pseudo-element on the chosen thumbnail. Ink circle, paper check, drawn with the owner’s own check mask.

Accessibility

Pattern: radio group (WAI-ARIA Authoring Practices), using native radio inputs so nothing is emulated. Without the script the strip is still a working radio group; the stage just does not follow.
