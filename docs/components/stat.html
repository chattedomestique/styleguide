Components

Stat

One number, big, with just enough around it to mean something. This page adds no CSS: a stat is the Card stat recipe, and this is how it sits with a Tag, a List, an Avatar and a Divider to make a dashboard.

The recipe

Four parts, in this order: what it counts (.card__eyebrow), the number (.card__figure), what state it is in (one tag, optional, or a meter), and a one-line reading of it (.card__meta). One state per card: a second fact goes in the meta line, not in a second tag. The figure scales with the card's own width, from 28 to 104px, stays on one line, and uses tabular figures. Square cards in one row share one height. Give the grid a small minimum (7.5rem here, 6.5rem inside the phone frame below) so a pair stays two to a row down to a 320px phone. A square card alone on its row (wider than 14rem) is as tall as its content.

A dashboard

The stat leads; the list is what to do next; the tags say where each row stands; the stack says who is in it. A labelled divider names each group. Everything is an element from the guide with its own markup; the only thing here that is new is the order.

Rules for composing

  • One number per card. If it needs two, it is two cards. The eyebrow says what is counted; the meta says what to make of it.
  • State goes in a tag, rhythm goes in a tone. A red card is not "bad". A tag that says Sync failed with a cross is.
  • The list is what to do next. Put the most urgent row first and give it a priority tag; a long list belongs on its own screen.
  • Write numbers for machines and people. .num or .card__figure for tabular figures, Intl.NumberFormat for the digits, and the true minus sign U+2212 for negatives.
  • Hide what the number already says. "5/12" is read as "5 of 12" if the /12 is decorative and the words are in a visually hidden span.
  • A dashboard has a beginning. Stats first, then what to do, then who is involved: four to eight elements, then stop.

Accessibility

  • Each stat is an article whose text reads in order: eyebrow, figure, meta. A meter has its own role="meter" with aria-valuenow; the figure next to it carries the same number as text.
  • Changing figures (a count that updates while you watch) are announced with SG.announce(), once per change, not on every tick.
  • Tags are text. A status tag carries a symbol and words, a priority carries bars and words, so nothing on the dashboard needs colour to be understood.
  • Headings stay in order: a screen title, then a divider heading for each group.

Parts used

Card (.card--square, .card__figure), Tag, List (.list, .kv), Avatar and stack, Divider and the Meter (.meter).
