Components

Player

A player puts Play, Back, Forward, a seek bar, the time and Mute under a recording. The recording is a native <audio> or <video>; the browser decodes it, buffers it and keeps its own controls, and the script only swaps those for ones that match the rest of the app. If the script never loads, the browser’s controls are all you see, and they work.

When to use

Do

Use it for a recording the person listens to or watches from start to finish: a voice memo, a lesson, a clip. Write what is in it (.player__desc, a transcript, or captions) so nobody has to press play to find out.

Don’t

Autoplay, or start sound on load. Do not put controls over the picture (they would need a scrim to be readable on every frame), and do not rename Play to Pause when it toggles: the name stays, the state is aria-pressed.

The media element has controls and the custom row is hidden; on load the script flips them. Press Play: the circle fills and its frame doubles, the icon becomes Pause, and the seek bar follows. Its name is still "Play".

Video

The picture sits above the controls and is closed off by a frame-weight rule, like a Card’s media. Clicking it plays and pauses (a pointer convenience; the button is the control). Nothing downloads until Play (preload="none"): the poster shows, and the length written in the markup stands until the recording reports its own, so a page of videos costs a phone nothing until one is played. This clip is silent, so there is no Mute, and its caption track says so. With real speech, add a real <track kind="captions">: the native controls carry the captions switch, so when they go, the Player brings it back as the Captions button, shown only when the media has captions.

Sunrise over the quay

A lighthouse on a headland; the sun rises over the water. 8 seconds, no sound.

States

Playing and muted are aria-pressed: filled and a doubled frame. Hovering or focusing Mute while it is off raises its outline but does not fill it, so it never looks muted under the pointer. Buffering is written in the status line after a beat, never by making Play inert. A failure is a dashed frame and a plain sentence with an icon (a second line hangs past the icon), the controls go aria-disabled, and the sentence’s “try again” is a real button under it: it reloads the recording and gives the controls back.

The first two players are pictures of a state: they hold no recording. The third is live. Its recording cannot be decoded, so the script fails it for real; press Try again and it asks for the recording once more, moves focus to Play, and fails again, saying so in the status line.

Anatomy

  1. Frame (.player, role="group" with a label): a 2px rectangle. data-skip sets the jump.
  2. Stage (.player__stage, video only): the picture at 16 / 9 (--ratio changes it), object-fit: cover.
  3. Head (.player__head): title and a sentence about what is in it. The description is the media element’s aria-describedby.
  4. Media (.player__media): the native element. Keeps controls until the script takes over. Audio uses preload="metadata" (its length comes from the file); a video with a poster can use preload="none" and write its length in the time, which stands until the media reports its own.
  5. Seek bar: a Scrubber (data-variant="plain", data-format="time"). Its label is visually hidden.
  6. Row (.player__row): one line at every size, every control centred on it. Play, Back and Forward are one group (.player__transport); Mute or Captions sit at the end (.player__end). The row is chrome: at 200% text it keeps its 100% size and the words around it grow. The script measures it and writes the richest layout that fits to data-fit: full, then compact (the skip pills become circles with the number inside the arrow), tight (Play at 44px too), small (every other control a 36px circle, 8px apart), and in a very narrow player bare (Play and Mute; the seek bar does the jumping). A player under 240px wide tightens its insets from 16 to 12px, so a phone-sized card keeps every control.
  7. Play (.player__play): the primary circle, 56px (44 in the tight and small layouts). Two icons, one shows.
  8. Back and Forward (data-player="back|forward"): 44px pills with the arrow and the number of seconds they jump (data-skip); in a narrower row, circles with the number inside the arrow, clear of the arrowhead. The arrow and the number read as one glyph, so neither grows with the text. The digit is visible and is in the name (“Back 5 seconds”), so the label matches.
  9. Time (.player__time): position and length, tabular, never broken, aria-hidden (the seek bar says it).
  10. Captions and Mute (data-player="captions|mute"): toggles at the end of the row, the same height as Back and Forward (44px, or 36px in the small layout). Every target in the row is 44px.
  11. Status (.player__status, role="status"): empty unless something needs saying. The icon is a column of its own, so a wrapped line hangs past it.
  12. Try again (data-player="retry"): only after a failure, under the sentence and lined up with its words. The script makes it if the markup has none.

API

Player attributes and behaviour
NameWhereEffect
data-skip.playerSeconds for Back and Forward (default 10). Write the same number in their aria-label.
controlsthe media elementLeave it in the markup. The script removes it only when it has taken over.
hidden.player__controlsLeave it in the markup. The script shows the row; without the script the native controls stay.
data-playera Buttonback, forward, mute, captions, retry. Play is .player__play.
data-fit.player__row (set by the script)full, compact, tight, small or bare: the richest layout that keeps the row on one line.
data-state.player (set by the script)buffering or error; absent otherwise. error dashes the frame.
--ratio.player__stagePicture shape, default 16 / 9.
SG.player.init(el)JSWire a player you added after load (existing ones are found automatically).

Accessibility

Player keyboard and screen reader behaviour
InteractionBehaviour
TabSeek bar, Play, Back, Forward, Captions, Mute, in the order they read (then Try again, after a failure). Every control is a native button or range.
Play: Enter / SpacePlays or pauses. The name stays "Play"; a screen reader hears "Play, toggle button, pressed" while it plays. Finishing is announced.
Seek: Left Right PageUp PageDown Home EndNative range keys, one second per step. The value is spoken as "0:06 of 0:12". Clicking the bar jumps there.
Back, ForwardJump data-skip seconds and announce the new position, since focus stays on the button.
Screen readerThe group is named by the title; the media element is described by the sentence under it. Icons are hidden; every button has a name. A failure is read from the status line; Try again is announced and moves focus to Play.
Captions and transcriptsVideo with speech needs a caption track (WCAG 1.2.2). Audio-only needs a transcript or an equivalent sentence (1.2.1). The Captions button appears only when there is a track.
TouchEvery target is 44px or has a 44px hit area, 8px apart. Nothing needs a drag: the bar jumps on tap, and the buttons step.
Zoom / text sizeThe title, description, time and status grow with the text; the row of controls keeps its 100% size and stays one line (measured), so it never breaks into rows of single buttons. Fine at 320px wide and 200% text.
Autoplay and soundNever autoplays. Starting one player pauses the others.
No scriptThe browser’s native controls remain and work.
Reduced motionNothing in the Player moves; the Button fills stay.
Forced coloursFrame and stage take CanvasText; buttons keep their borders; pressed is a 4px frame.
