Style guide

One system for every app you build: minimal, brutalist, friendly, and clear about what to do next. It grows one element at a time; this is where they live.

Where it comes from

This guide is the owner's Flashcards style sheet v0.1, generalised. The type, space, line, shape, hit-target and focus tokens, the role names (--paper, --ink, --tone-1…6), the .card element and its ten variants, the gates and the checker rules are theirs. Added on top: real colour (dark mode, high contrast, six palettes) with contrast verified in a browser, an accessibility test gate, and the elements that came from the reference screens.

The seven rules

  1. Two line weights. 2px frames what you press or what contains. 1px only divides. 4px exists for forced colours and nothing else.
  2. Flat. No gradients, no blur, no glass. A shadow is hard-edged and means "you can press this".
  3. Rectangles hold, circles act. Containers are rectangles (square or soft). Buttons, handles and action slots are circles or pills.
  4. Uppercase is structure. Bar labels, index numbers, the display line. Everything you read is sentence case.
  5. Say something real. No lorem ipsum, no "John Doe", no "seamless". Copy comes from the app.
  6. Colour is a swap. Components read roles. Only src/tokens contains a colour literal.
  7. No decoration. If it carries no information and offers no action, it goes.

Use it in an app

<html lang="en" data-palette="mint" data-corners="soft">
  <link rel="stylesheet" href="styleguide.css">
  <button class="btn" data-variant="primary">Add funds</button>
