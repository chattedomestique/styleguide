Agenda

An agenda says what is on and when. Two shapes, both built on the Card: a list of days, each a tonal card whose busy hours are columns, and a single day laid out against the clock, where events that overlap sit side by side or, when there is no room, stack in time order.

When to use

Do

Use the days list for "what is this week like?" and the time grid for "what does Friday look like?". Let every event state its own start and end as text; the ruler is only a guide.

Don't

Show more than three overlapping events side by side, use a tone to say what kind of event it is (tones carry rhythm, not meaning), or make people drag an event to move it without a button that does the same.

Examples

Days, with hour columns

Each day is a Card with a tone. Only the hours that have something are shown, and an all-day event is a slot whose label says so (Friday's rent). On a phone the hours are rows; once the card is 34rem wide they become columns with 1px rules between, and the date becomes a rail (the query is on the card, not the screen). On a narrow card (200% text on a phone) each hour sits on its own line above its events, so a chip keeps the card's whole width. Every chip says its title, then its time under it. Two events in the same hour stack: Friday's 14:00 column holds two. The chips take the card's tone, darker, and lift when pressed like a Button. A day with nothing on says so.

Event states

A chip is a link, so it lifts on hover and on keyboard focus (same lift). Pressed, it lies flat and its fill takes 12% of its own ink at once, so a tap on a phone shows straight away (the words keep 7:1 on the tint). The day card itself is not pressable, so it never lifts. The is-* classes only force a state for this page.

One day, against the clock

Hours run down the side; an event is a tonal Card as tall as it lasts. The grid has a line every 15 minutes, and each event says where it starts and ends with --from and --to. Where events overlap (design review and the slide deck, study block and the landlord's call) they take lanes; where one ends as the next begins (the standup and the review), a 2px gap keeps their frames from fusing into one heavy line. Each event is a whole-card link with one tab stop, so its hit area is the whole card. A quarter hour is 2rem, so even a half-hour event in a narrow lane holds its title on two lines and its time line under them; a title that would need a third line is cut with an ellipsis in the grid, and the link's name still carries all of it.

Anatomy

  1. Day card (.card.agenda__day): the owner's Card with a tone. The body is a grid that changes with the card's width.
  2. Date (.agenda__date): weekday label and a big tabular numeral with the month, in a <time>. A rail at 34rem.
  3. Hour (.agenda__slot, .agenda__hour): a row, or a column with 1px rules. Only busy hours exist.
  4. Event chip (.agenda__event): a link in the card's darker tone (--tone-fill, text --tone-on-fill), 2px frame, with its start and end.
  5. Time grid (.agenda-day__grid): a 15-minute row grid. The ruler (.agenda-day__hours) draws 1px lines; the events (.agenda-day__events) sit on the same rows in lanes.
  6. Event card (.card.card--link.agenda-day__event): a tonal link card placed by --from, --to, --lane. Title, then the times.

Accessibility

Agenda keyboard and screen reader behaviour
InteractionBehaviour
TabOne stop per event, in time order (the DOM order is the time order, whatever the lane). The day card and the ruler take none. In a card the ring sits inside it; on a chip it follows the chip.
EnterFollows the event's link. A whole-card link needs no second target.
Screen readerEach day is a card labelled by its date (<time>); the events are a list; each event is a link whose name is the title followed by its start and end. The hour ruler is aria-hidden because every event states its own times.
ColourTone is rhythm: two days can share a tone and mean the same. Every fact (day, time, title) is text. Chip text is --tone-on-fill, 7:1 on the chip (checked by the contrast matrix).
TouchChips and event cards are at least 44px. A 30-minute event at the default scale is 48px tall. Nothing is moved by dragging.
Zoom / text sizeSizes are rem. The hours reflow from columns to rows at 34rem of card width, and lanes stack below 16rem (two) or 26rem (three), so 200% text trades the time scale for legibility.
Reduced motionThe only motion is the chip or card lifting on hover and focus; its travel is multiplied by --move, so it becomes a hard shadow appearing.
Forced coloursFills go; chips and cards keep their 2px frames, get a 4px Highlight outline on hover and focus, and the ruler's lines become CanvasText.
