Calendar

A calendar answers "what day?" and "what is on it?". The month is a grid of seven columns you can cross with the arrow keys; the chosen day's events are listed below it as text. Week start, month names and weekday names come from Intl.

When to use

Do

Use it to pick or browse a day when context matters (what else is that week?). Use the week strip when the screen is short. Put the events in text below; the markers only say "something is on".

Don't

Use it to type a birthday or a date far away (a date field is faster: <input type="date">), colour days by meaning, or hide the event list behind a hover.

Examples

Month

Today (30 September here, pinned with data-today so this page does not change with the date) has a ring and an underlined numeral. The chosen day, the 2nd, is filled and its frame doubles. The squares under a numeral count events up to three; the day's name says the count in words and the list below says what they are. Days of the neighbouring months are quieter, not faint, because they are real buttons. Try the arrow keys, Home, End, PageUp and PageDown.

Week starts on Sunday

No data-first-day: the week start comes from the locale (Intl.Locale week info), and the names follow it. en-US starts on Sunday; set data-first-day="1" to force Monday for your app.

Week strip

One row of seven tall days, for a screen that shares space with a list. Same buttons, same keys; PageUp and PageDown move a week, and the two round buttons page by week. The weekday names are part of each day's button. Every day keeps at least 44px: when seven do not fit (a small phone, or 200% text), the strip scrolls sideways and snaps to a day, the chosen day is scrolled into view, and the day cut at the frame's edge says there is more. It never squeezes the days until their dates collide.

A range

data-min and data-max make the days outside them unavailable: a faint numeral struck through, no frame, not choosable, but still reachable by keyboard and named "not available", so nobody is left guessing why. They are quiet on purpose: the days you can choose are the point of the grid.

Another language

Nothing is translated by hand: month, weekday and date names are Intl. The strings the calendar adds itself ("today", "events", the button names) are in SG.calendar.labels; replace them to translate.

States

A day is chosen, like a chip or a tab, so it follows the selection-control recipe: the solid fill belongs to the chosen day alone. Each state has a cue that is not colour: hover and focus lift the day and draw its ring, without filling it; pressing puts it back with a light tint, at once; today is ringed, heavy and underlined; the chosen day is filled and framed twice, and stays filled when pressed; "Other" is a day of a neighbouring month (quieter, not faint); "Off" is unavailable (a faint numeral struck through, no frame). Under the numeral, one, two or three-plus event markers. The is-* classes only force a state for this page.

Anatomy

  1. Frame (.cal): 2px, paper, the card radius. A size container.
  2. Head (.cal__head): previous and next as round Buttons, and the month (.cal__title), a polite live region so paging is announced. One row at every size: at large text the month wraps between the two buttons.
  3. Weekday bar (thead): the ink bar with uppercase names. abbr carries the long name; below 15rem wide, one letter.
  4. Day (.cal__day): a circle of up to 2.5rem inside a 44px-tall cell; the hit area is the whole cell. Its name is the full date. In narrow columns (200% text on a phone) the numerals take Archivo's condensed width, so two digits stay inside their circle at full size.
  5. Today: a 2px ring and an underlined, heavy numeral.
  6. Chosen day (td[aria-selected="true"]): filled with the accent, frame doubled, numeral in paper.
  7. Markers (.cal__pips): one to three 4px squares under the numeral (just under the circle when the column is very narrow). Decorative.
  8. Events (.cal__events): the chosen day's title and a list of time + title, as text, each row in its tone. The times share one column, so the titles line up; in a narrow calendar every time sits over its title.

Accessibility

Calendar keyboard and screen reader behaviour
InteractionBehaviour
TabPrevious, next, then one stop in the grid (the focused day; roving tabindex), then on.
Arrow keysLeft and right: a day (mirrored in right-to-left). Up and down: a week. Crossing a month edge pages the grid and keeps focus on the new day.
Home / EndThe first or last day of the week (per the week start).
PageUp / PageDownThe same day in the previous or next month (week view: a week). With Shift: a year (week view: a month).
Enter / SpaceChoose the day; its events are read out.
Screen readerA grid with column headers (full names via abbr). A day reads "Wednesday 30 September 2026, today, 2 events"; the chosen cell is "selected". Paging announces the new month. The markers are hidden because the count is in the name and the list below is text.
TouchEvery day has a full-cell hit area: 44px tall in the month, taller in the week strip, and a seventh of the calendar wide (about 47px on a 390px screen, 40 to 43px on 320px with 16px gutters). Seven columns need a 308px container to reach 44px each, so a smaller container (a phone-sized mock on this site, a narrow pane) gets narrower cells, over the 24px floor down to about 11rem. The cells touch, so there is no dead zone between two days to mis-tap into (the Carousel's dots do the same). Paging is a button, not a swipe.
Zoom / text sizeThe numerals and words grow; the 44px rows, the insets and the event markers do not (they are chrome). The circle follows the column, so 200% text keeps seven columns: numerals turn condensed in narrow columns instead of shrinking, weekday names drop to one letter below 15rem, and the week strip scrolls rather than squeeze its days.
Reduced motionThe only motion is a day's lift on hover and focus, multiplied by --move; it becomes a frame appearing. Paging is instant.
Forced coloursEvery day keeps a ButtonText frame, the chosen day a 4px one, focus a 4px Highlight outline; today is still underlined; an unavailable day is GrayText and struck through, with no frame; markers use CanvasText.
