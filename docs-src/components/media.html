Media

A picture, in a frame, with a caption if it needs one. The picture is content, so the frame holds it in a rectangle; the only text that ever touches a photo sits on a solid plate, so contrast never depends on what the photo looks like.

When to use

Do

Use it for any photo, illustration or video that the person came to look at. Pick one ratio per screen, give every picture an alt that says what it shows, and use a caption for what the picture cannot say: when, where, who.

Don’t

Put words on a bare photo, or bake words into the picture: a screenshot of a sentence cannot be resized, translated or read aloud. For a card with a picture on top, use Card; for many pictures, use a Gallery or a Carousel.

Examples

Ratios

The frame has a fixed shape and the picture is cropped to it (object-fit: cover), so a column of photos lines up whatever shape the files were. Set the ratio with data-ratio, and the focal point with --pos when the subject is not in the middle. free drops the crop and uses the file’s own shape; give the img its width and height so the page does not jump when it loads.

Landscape, wide, then square and portrait: in each row the pictures share one height, so their widths compare the ratios. Each caption is the figure’s data-ratio; free here is the file’s own 4:3.

Crop and fit

--pos moves the crop: here 10% 50%, the default 50% 50%, and 92% 50%. data-fit="contain", the last, shows the whole picture on a --paper-2 mat, for logos and product shots where cropping would cut something off.

Captions

A caption is a <figcaption>, so the figure is named by it. Three kinds, chosen with data-caption; a data-tone on the figure tints the plain strip and the space behind the picture while it loads.

The three caption kinds
data-captionWhat it isUse it for
plain (default)A paper strip under the picture, closed off by a 2px rule. Tone-able.Most things. Longer captions; anything you may want to select and copy.
barThe same strip, solid ink with paper text: the frame made thicker, like the bar on a card.A date and a place, editorial tiles, a strong column of pictures.
overA flat --scrim-strong band across the bottom of the picture, with --on-media text.Small tiles, where a strip under the picture would eat the picture. The snow above is the worst case: near-white, and the caption still reads at 8.5:1.
Why the plate is not a fade

The band is one flat colour at 75% black, so its contrast is the same everywhere: white text over it is at least 8.5:1 even on a pure white photo. A fade from clear to dark would be strongest where the text is not and weakest where the photo is brightest, and the house has no gradients anyway.

States

An image has no attribute that says "still loading" or "gave up", so the state is data-state on the figure. src/js/70-media.js sets it from the image’s own load and error events; without the script the picture just appears. The frame keeps its ratio the whole time, so nothing around it moves; only when the words need more room than the ratio gives (large text, a narrow tile) does it grow, because the words are never cut off.

  • Loading is a ring that spins (a slow pulse under reduced motion) and a sentence. The figure is aria-busy. When the picture arrives it fades in over --dur-enter.
  • Failed is a dashed frame (the house legend for "not here"), an icon, a sentence and a way out. Status is always icon and words. The script also announces the sentence politely, because nothing moved focus.

Here is the real thing: an image that cannot decode, caught by the script, with the sentence and the button it built. Press Try again: the picture is requested again, fails again, and the figure says so once more, politely, without moving you.

A figure that is one link

When the whole figure goes somewhere, put data-link on it and class="media__link" on the anchor in the title. The anchor’s ::after stretches over the figure: one tab stop, one name, and the hit area is the whole thing. It lifts like a pressable thing, because it is one; the focus ring goes around the figure, not the text. Alt text

Alt text is for the person who cannot see the picture; the caption is for everyone. Do not paste one into the other, and do not write "image of". The test: read the page aloud with the pictures turned into their alt text. Does it still make sense, and is nothing said twice?

How to write alt text for each kind of picture
The picture is…So the alt is…For example
Information: the reason it is on the pageOne sentence about what matters in it, not what it is made of.alt="Three small boats on flat water under a low orange sun"
Already described by the caption next to itEmpty, so it is not read twice. The figure is still named by its caption.alt="" beside a caption that says "Tomato stall, number 14"
Decoration: a texture, a dividerEmpty, and better still, not an image at all.alt=""
A link or a buttonWhere it goes or what it does, not what it shows.alt="Open photo: Snow on the pass"
A chart, map or diagramA short name for it, and the detail in text next to it (aria-describedby or a table).alt="Steps per day, last week" plus a table of the seven values
A picture of textThe same words. Better: do not do this (WCAG 1.4.5). Real text resizes, translates and is read aloud.A screenshot of a menu becomes a list
Video and sound

A <video> goes inside the frame like an image. Keep its native controls, add a captions track (<track kind="captions">), and never start it with sound. Moving pictures that start on their own and last more than five seconds need a visible pause (WCAG 2.2.2).

Square and soft corners

The figure reads --radius-tile, so data-corners changes it with no code in the component. The picture is clipped by the frame, the caption strip by the same frame, and the figure’s own background is the frame’s colour, so no light hairline shows inside a soft corner between the frame and an ink caption bar.

Anatomy

  1. Figure .media: a 2px frame in --line, --radius-tile, clipped. It is a size container, so it needs a width from its parent (a grid cell, a block), exactly like Card.
  2. Frame .media__frame: the ratio box. The picture is placed absolutely inside it, so the ratio alone decides the height.
  3. Picture: an img, video, canvas, svg or picture. Always has alt (or is a video with captions).
  4. Badge .media__badge (optional): a pill label in the corner on a scrim plate.
  5. Status .media__status: loading or failed, centred over the frame. Hidden unless data-state is set.
  6. Caption .media__caption (optional): a <figcaption> with .media__label (uppercase, structure), .media__title and .media__meta (sentence case).

Accessibility

Media keyboard and screen reader behaviour
InteractionBehaviour
TabA plain figure is not a tab stop, because it does nothing. A data-link figure has one (its anchor); a failed one has one (Try again). The ring follows the figure, inside the frame where the frame clips.
Screen reader"Figure, [caption]". The image is read with its alt. Loading is aria-busy; a failure is announced politely once.
ContrastCaption text is --ink / --ink-soft on paper (7:1 and up), paper on ink in a bar (its softer second line measured at 8.4:1 in the worst theme and palette), --on-media on --scrim-strong over a photo (8.5:1 at worst). On a tone the tone’s own --tone-ink / --tone-ink-soft apply (the soft one is guaranteed 4.5:1, nearer 5 in the palest palettes). No text is ever placed on a bare photo.
TouchA linked figure’s hit area is the whole figure. "Try again" is 36px drawn, 44px hit area.
Zoom / text sizeCaptions are rem and wrap between words (overflow-wrap: break-word). The insets around them and the status icon are chrome and stop at their 100% size, so at 200% text the words keep the room. A badge is chrome as a whole, its word included: it sits on the picture, and a plate that grew with the text would cover what the person came to see. Figures under 192px get a tighter caption through a container query; nothing clips at 200% text.
Reduced motionNo lift travel; the fill still changes. The picture fades in (opacity is safe). The loading ring pulses instead of spinning.
Forced coloursThe frame becomes CanvasText. Scrims and fills are replaced by the system palette, so the over caption gets a 2px rule above it. A hovered or focused link figure gets a 4px Highlight outline.
