Toast

A toast confirms something the person just did: saved, copied, sent, deleted. It appears above the dock, says its piece, and leaves. It never takes focus, never blocks the screen, and pauses while you are reading it.

When to use

Do

Confirm the result of an action the person may not have seen: Link copied, Deck saved, Card deleted with Undo. Keep it to one short sentence.

Don't

Put the only copy of anything important in a toast: a failed payment, a validation error, a deadline. Use an alert or an inline message, and let the toast repeat it.

Accessibility

A toast is a status message (WCAG 4.1.3): it is announced without moving focus. Anything that needs a decision belongs in a dialog; anything that should still be there in a minute belongs in an alert.

Examples

Status

Press each button. The plain one and the statuses close themselves; the error stays until you dismiss it, because a timer must not take away the only chance to read what failed. Long text shows a two-line message, which stays longer (see the duration rule below).

With an action

One action at most, and it must also be reachable somewhere else (a menu, the item itself), because keyboard and screen-reader users reach a toast last. A toast with an action never closes itself.

Duration grows with the message

Nobody reads at a constant speed, and a second language or a small screen makes it slower. The default is 3 s + 75 ms per character, never below 6 s and never above 20 s. Pausing on hover, focus or touch-hold adds what you need.

Stacking

At most three toasts are visible. The newest wins: the oldest toast that would have closed itself makes room. Toasts that cannot close themselves (errors, actions) are never dropped; a new one waits in a queue instead.

Static markup

The same classes work without JavaScript, for a server-rendered flash message. Add data-dismissible and data-sg-dismiss to a real button to get a working close. The plain toast is data-tone="ink"; each status has its own icon shape, so colour is never the only cue.

Toast, or just announce?

Both tell a screen reader something happened without moving focus. The difference is whether a sighted person sees it too.

Choosing between SG.announce, toast, alert and dialog
WhenUse, and why
The screen already shows the change: a counter went from 3 to 2, a list re-sorted, "12 results", a tab switchedSG.announce("12 results"). Silent on screen, spoken once. A visible toast would repeat what is already in front of the person.
The result is off screen or easy to miss: copied, saved, sent, deletedSG.toast.show(). A visible confirmation that is spoken too. Add action for Undo.
Something failed and the person must knowAn inline message or an alert, plus an error toast. The toast is the quick signal; the inline message is the record that stays and sits next to the thing to fix.
A decision is needed before continuingA dialog. It needs focus and a keyboard trap that is left on purpose. A toast must never ask a question.

SG.toast has its own two live regions, so you do not also call SG.announce() for the same message. Errors use the assertive one; everything else is polite.

Timing and behaviour

Toast timing rules
SituationWhat happens
DefaultCloses after 3 s + 75 ms/character, between 6 and 20 s. A shorter duration is raised to 6 s.
Pointer over the toastThe timer pauses. On leaving, at least 1.5 s remain.
Keyboard focus inside the toastThe timer pauses until focus leaves it.
Finger held on the toastThe timer pauses until you lift (touch has no hover).
Tab hidden or app in the backgroundThe timer pauses; the person cannot have read it.
status: "danger", an action, or persistent: trueNever closes itself. Dismiss button, the action, or Esc on the focused toast.
Same id, or same text and statusUpdated in place or extended, not stacked twice.
A fourth arrivesThe oldest self-closing toast leaves. If all three are persistent the new one queues.

Anatomy

  1. Region: one <section> labelled “Notifications” (aria-label) in the top layer (a manual popover), above modal dialogs, lifted by --dock-h and --safe-bottom. Its empty strip never intercepts taps.
  2. Surface: a rectangle with a 2px frame (--radius-card). The plain toast is ink with paper text, so it stands apart from the page in light and dark; a status toast takes the tone's pastel. No shadow: it is not pressable.
  3. Status icon (optional): a 24px mask. The shape differs per status; the hue is rhythm on top.
  4. Message: one sentence, sentence case, semibold, with a visually hidden status word for screen readers.
  5. Action (optional): one small button in the toast's tone.
  6. Dismiss: a small circle button with a 44×44px hit area and an aria-label. Always present, because touch users have no Esc.
  7. Live regions: two visually hidden regions, polite and assertive, created at page load. The announcement goes here; the visible toast is not a live region, so the Undo button is not read out as if it were prose.

Accessibility

Toast keyboard and screen reader behaviour
InteractionBehaviour
Screen readerThe message is spoken through a persistent live region: polite, or assertive for errors ("Error: Could not save"). An action is announced as "Undo is available in notifications". Focus does not move (WCAG 4.1.3).
F8Jumps to the newest toast's first button from anywhere. Focus returns to where it was when the toast closes.
Tab, Shift+TabThe region is a labelled landmark at the end of the page; its buttons are ordinary tab stops.
Enter / SpacePresses the focused action or dismiss button.
EscOn a focused toast: dismisses it and returns focus. It does not close a dialog behind it.
Hover, focus, touch-holdEach pauses the auto-dismiss timer (WCAG 2.2.1 Timing Adjustable; nothing is lost while reading).
TouchDismiss and action have a 44×44px hit area with 8px between.
Focus ringOn the plain (ink) toast the ring is paper and its halo ink, so it follows the surface. On a status tone the page's ring is used.
Text sizeThe message grows with the reader's text size, and so does the status icon, which leads its first line (1.25 times the message's size, never under 24px: 40px at 200%). The insets and the 36px dismiss circle stop at their 100% size, so at 200% the sentence keeps the room. The icon and the dismiss circle stay level with the message's first line, and the action drops under the words, never under the icon. Where the words beside them would still be narrower than their longest word (a narrow panel, 200% text on a small phone), the dismiss circle moves down to the action row, for every toast of the stack alike, so a word never breaks inside itself.
Reduced motionThe rise is replaced by a fade (--move is 0); the exit is a fade too.
Forced coloursThe toast keeps a CanvasText frame; each button keeps its own border; icons are CanvasText.
  • Never the only place critical information appears. A failed save is also shown next to the thing that failed.
  • Keep Undo (or Retry) reachable elsewhere too: a menu item, the list itself. Toasts are the last thing in the tab order.
  • While a modal dialog is open the page behind it is inert, and so are toasts (they are outside the dialog). They are still announced; hold action toasts until the dialog closes.
  • Known limit: a toast can cover content near the bottom edge while it is visible (WCAG 2.4.11). Keep messages short, at most three, and remember focus pauses the timer, not the layout.
