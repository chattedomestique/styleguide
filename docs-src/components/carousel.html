Carousel

A short set of pictures or cards you look at one at a time. The slides are a native scroll strip, so a swipe works with no script; the buttons, dots and counter sit in a bar underneath, where they never depend on what the photo looks like. It does not move by itself unless you ask it to, and then it always has a pause.

When to use

Do

Use it for a small, finite set that belongs together: the photos of one entry, the stops on a trail, the steps of a tour. Six or fewer is ideal; past eight, use a Gallery or a list. Give every slide the same ratio so the height never jumps.

Don’t

Hide something people must read on slide four, or use a carousel for a promotion that cycles on its own. If it moves, it needs a pause (WCAG 2.2.2). If a person has to go looking, put it on the page.

Examples

Basic

Swipe, drag, scroll, press the buttons, or use the dots. The counter and the dots follow the scroll position, so they are right whichever way you moved. Every slide is named "n of N".

Peek

data-peek makes each slide 84% wide so the next one shows at the edge. That is a cue, never the only way on: the buttons are still there. Slides keep their own frames here. This one has no dots: the counter and buttons are the smallest useful set.

1 of 5

Loop

data-loop lets Previous on the first slide go to the last and Next on the last go to the first. The buttons are then never disabled. Without it, the buttons say aria-disabled at the ends (not disabled, so focus is never lost when you reach the last slide). The next example loops.

Auto-advance

data-autoplay="3500" adds a Play button and moves to the next slide every 3.5 seconds once it is pressed. It is off until the person asks. Add data-autoplay-start to begin playing on load. The rules that keep it safe are in the script, not left to you:

  • It needs a .carousel__play button. Without one it logs a warning and stays off: WCAG 2.2.2 wants a visible way to pause anything that moves for more than five seconds.
  • It never starts, and stops if already running, under reduced motion.
  • It stops for good when focus enters the carousel or the track is touched. It pauses (and resumes) while the pointer rests on it or the tab is hidden.
  • It never announces slides. The button announces itself: "Slide show started" or "paused", and its name changes between "Start slide show" and "Pause slide show".

States

The dots are the state worth seeing on their own. The solid fill is kept for the current dot, and it also stretches into a pill, so it is never just a darker dot; in forced colours the fill goes and the long, heavy outline stays. Hover draws the dot’s 44px cell as a ring, keyboard focus is the focus ring, and a press tints the dot half way at once, so a tap shows before the slide has moved. None of them fills a dot: a filled dot means “here”.

Anatomy

  1. Frame .carousel: a <section> with aria-roledescription="carousel" and a label. 2px, --radius-card, clipped, a size container.
  2. Track .carousel__track: a native horizontal scroller with scroll-snap-type: x mandatory. No scrollbar; swipe, trackpad and Shift+wheel work with no script. It is tabindex="0" with role="group" and a name, so a keyboard can reach it.
  3. Slide .carousel__slide: role="group", aria-roledescription="slide", aria-label="3 of 6". Holds a Media figure, or anything else.
  4. Bar .carousel__bar: paper, closed off by the 2px rule. One row: the count, then the circles at the end. Hidden when scripting is off, so there are no controls that do nothing.
  5. Count .carousel__count: “3 of 6” in the label style, tabular figures. Put the words in .carousel__of: when the count and the circles cannot share the line, it shortens to “3/6” (the words stay for a screen reader), and only then goes.
  6. Dots .carousel__dots > .carousel__dot (optional): a group of buttons, the current one aria-current="true". Their own row, centred, below 40rem; between the count and the circles from 40rem. Hidden as a group when one line cannot hold them.
  7. Nav .carousel__nav: optional .carousel__play, then .carousel__prev and .carousel__next (Buttons, circles).

Accessibility

Pattern: WAI-ARIA Authoring Practices, "Carousel". Two differences from the example, on purpose: all slides stay in the accessibility tree (they are a scroll strip, so a screen-reader user can read ahead), and a change the person made is announced with a polite message instead of a live region on the slides.
