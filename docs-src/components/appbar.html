App bar

The app bar sits at the top of a screen and answers three questions: where am I, how do I go back, and what can I do here. Its title is the page heading.

When to use

Do

One bar per screen, with the screen's name as its <h1>. Say where Back goes (Back to Inbox), keep to one or two actions at the end, and give every icon-only button a name.

Don't

Put the app's main navigation here (that is the dock), use more than two actions (a menu holds the rest), or use it for in-page sections (tabs).

Examples

Standard

Back at the start, the title filling the middle, an action at the end. The bar is flat: a 2px rule under it and no shadow. The title is a bar label (uppercase is structure); the back link names its destination. Every control is a button, so it lifts and fills like one.

Account

Ink and tonal

data-tone on the bar sets its colours: ink is the inverted strip the owner's card bar uses (paper text on ink), 1 to 6 a hue. The buttons inside invert with it, and the focus ring follows the bar, so it is never ink-on-ink.

Search

Back, a search field, an add button. The field is a real <input type="search"> with a name (visually hidden here, because the bar's icon and placeholder carry it for sighted users), 16px text so iOS does not zoom, and a 44px height. The form has role="search".

Sticky, and out of the way of focus

With data-sticky the bar stays at the top while the content scrolls, pads the status bar with --safe-top, and bleeds to the screen edges of .app. A sticky bar covers content, and WCAG 2.4.11 says a focused control must never be hidden behind it. So src/js/41-bars.js measures the bar and publishes --appbar-h, and the foundation puts it in scroll-padding. Tab through the list below: each row scrolls into view below the bar. (The page scrolls in <html> in a real app, where the foundation's scroll-padding does the work; this frame scrolls itself, so it repeats that one line.) In an app

<meta name="viewport"
      content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, viewport-fit=cover">

<div class="app">
  <header class="appbar" data-sticky>…</header>
  <main class="app__main">…</main>
</div>

The bar's titles in this page are <h4> only so they sit under the page's own headings; in an app the page title is the <h1>. viewport-fit=cover lets the page draw under the status bar, and the bar's --safe-top padding keeps the controls out of it. On iOS standalone the inset can report 0; nothing here depends on it being right.

Anatomy

  1. Bar (.appbar, a <header>): 54px with the small buttons (36px, 8px above and below), a 2px rule under it. Paper-coloured, or the colours of data-tone.
  2. Start: Back or Menu, a circle button with an aria-label that says where it goes.
  3. Title (.appbar__title): the page heading. Uppercase bar label; the greeting uses a sentence. Wraps, never truncates.
  4. Eyebrow (greeting only): a soft-ink line above the title.
  5. End (.appbar__end): one or two circle buttons.
  6. Search field (search only): a labelled type="search" input with a leading icon, 44px tall.
  7. Safe area (sticky): --safe-top above the row, --safe-left / --safe-right at the sides.

Accessibility

App bar keyboard and screen reader behaviour
InteractionBehaviour
TabVisual order is DOM order: Back, (title), actions. The editor bar is Cancel, title, Help, Apply. The ring is 3px and follows the bar's colours.
Enter / SpaceActivate the button; Enter follows a link; Enter in the search field submits.
Screen readerA banner landmark when the header is a child of body; the page heading "Account, heading level 1"; "Back to Inbox, link"; "Notifications, 2 new, button"; the search form is a search landmark with a labelled field.
Touch44×44px targets (the small buttons are drawn at 36 with an invisible extension), 8px apart.
Zoom / text sizeThe bar wraps instead of squeezing: the title and the search field keep at least 6rem, and what does not fit drops to a second row with the actions at the end. At 200% text the title wraps by whole words; nothing is truncated or clipped, and a sticky bar stays about two rows tall, not half the screen.
Right-to-leftThe bar mirrors, and Back arrows (ic--arrow-left, ic--arrow-right) flip to point the right way.
Reduced motionButtons raise without travel.
Forced coloursThe bar gets a CanvasText rule, buttons keep their borders, the field is Field.
  • Every icon-only button has an aria-label that names the action or destination ("Back to Inbox", not "Back", not "arrow").
  • The title is the page's only <h1>. Inside a sheet or dialog it is the dialog's heading (<h2>, referenced by aria-labelledby).
  • Keep the bar's contents the same on sibling screens (WCAG 3.2.3 Consistent Navigation).
  • A placeholder is not a label: the search field has its own (visually hidden) <label>.

Icons. arrow-left, pencil, menu, search, ellipsis, bell and circle-help come from dist/icons.css; the main bundle ships only plus, minus, close, check and arrow. Link icons.css in your app, or define the masks yourself: an .ic with no mask paints a solid square.
