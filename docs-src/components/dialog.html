Dialog

A dialog stops the flow to ask for one decision. It is a native <dialog> wearing the card's frame, so the browser does the hard parts (it traps focus, blocks the page behind it, closes on Esc) and the look stays the owner's: a 2px frame, an ink bar, a flat scrim behind.

When to use

Do

Use a dialog for a decision that cannot wait: confirm something destructive, leave with unsaved changes, answer one short question such as a new name. Put the verb in the button: Delete deck, not OK.

Don't

Use a dialog to inform. It interrupts on purpose, so news, tips and "saved" messages belong inline. Do not ask to confirm something that can be undone: do it and offer Undo. Never open a second dialog on top of the first.

Dialogs interrupt

Every dialog costs the person their place on the screen. Ask once, ask clearly. For content to browse or act on at length, use a sheet. For a hint next to a control, use a popover.

Form

One short question. Wrap bar, body and actions in a <form method="dialog">: the browser validates, closes the dialog and reports which button was pressed in returnValue. The first field has autofocus. Cancel is a plain type="button" with data-sg-close, so it skips validation, and because Save is the form's only submit button, Enter in the field saves.

Required

data-required switches off Esc and the scrim tap, and leaves out the close control, for the rare moment when the person must choose before anything else can happen (the session ended, a payment needs a decision). Longer content

The bar and the actions stay put; the body scrolls. If the body holds nothing focusable, SG makes it a keyboard-reachable region named after the dialog. A close control in the bar is friendly when there is no obvious "No" (touch screens have no Esc). data-size="lg" widens it to 40rem.

Sharing

How sharing works

When you share a deck, everyone you invite can study it and sees the cards in your order. They cannot see your other decks, your streak, or anything you have archived.

You stay the owner. Removing a person takes effect on their device the next time it connects, and anything they already marked as known stays theirs.

Invitations expire after seven days. A link works for the person it was sent to, on up to three devices. If you think a link reached someone it was not meant for, stop sharing and invite again: old links stop working at once.

Shared decks sync whenever you are online and are kept on each device, so they keep working without a connection. Deleting a shared deck deletes it for everyone, after a warning.

Questions about how your information is stored are answered in the privacy notice. It is written in plain language and lists every place your data goes.

With a tone

A dialog is a card, so data-tone works: the body takes the tone, and ink flips the bar to paper. Tones carry rhythm, never meaning: what makes this a good-news dialog is the word and the icon in the bar.

Without the JavaScript hooks

Anatomy

  1. Dialog: <dialog class="dialog card">. The owner's card in the top layer: 2px frame, --radius-card, no shadow. 28rem wide at most (data-size), never wider than the screen minus a 16px margin and the safe areas.
  2. Bar (.card__bar): names the kind of dialog in uppercase (Confirm, Rename, Signed out), with an icon when it is a status, and holds the close control when there is one.
  3. Title (.card__title, a real heading, h2 by default): a sentence-case question the person can answer. aria-labelledby points at it.
  4. Body (.card__body, .card__text): the consequence, or the fields. Scrolls on its own when long.
  5. Actions (.dialog__actions): pills, primary last. On a phone-width dialog they stack full width in the same order.
  6. Scrim: --scrim (60% black), the one translucency the system allows. With the 2px frame it is the only thing that separates the dialog from the page.

Accessibility

Dialog keyboard and screen reader behaviour
InteractionBehaviour
Tab / Shift+TabCycles through the controls inside the dialog only. Everything behind it is inert (WCAG 2.1.2: never a trap, because Esc and a button always leave).
EscCloses (same as the dismissing button) and returns focus to the opener. Not in a data-required dialog, which always has a button for every way out.
Enter / SpaceActivates the focused button. In a form dialog Enter in a field submits with the default button.
Initial focusDestructive: Cancel. Confirm: the primary button. Form: the first field. Long reading: the primary button, and the body is also reachable by keyboard. Set with autofocus; with none, the browser picks the first control (the close control).
Focus order (2.4.3)DOM order is visual order, also when the actions stack on a phone. Nothing is reversed with CSS.
Screen readerAnnounced as "Title, dialog" (or "alert dialog") followed by the description. The title is a real heading, so heading navigation works. The bar's icon is hidden; its words are read.
TouchButtons are 44px tall with 8px between them. The scrim tap and every button are taps; nothing needs a gesture.
Zoom / text sizeWidth and padding are rem, the body scrolls, long words wrap. The dialog never exceeds the screen and stays inside the safe areas, also at 200% text and 320px.
Reduced motionThe 24px rise is multiplied by --move, so only a fade remains (180ms, never instant).
Forced coloursThe card's frame becomes CanvasText; the bar gets a real line under it because its ink fill is gone; buttons keep their borders.
  • Every dialog needs a title the person can see and aria-labelledby pointing at it. An unlabelled dialog is announced as just "dialog".
  • The status icon in the bar is never the only signal: the bar's words and the title say what is at stake.
  • Do not put tabindex on the <dialog>. Use autofocus on a control inside it.
  • A dialog opened from a menu item returns focus to the menu's button, because the item is hidden by then.
