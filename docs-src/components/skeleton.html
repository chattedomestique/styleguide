Skeleton

A skeleton is a grey sketch of the content that is on its way: same places, same sizes, so the page does not jump when the real thing lands. It shows only if the wait is long enough to notice, it never shimmers, and it holds perfectly still for people who ask for less motion.

When to use

Choosing a loading indicator by wait length
The wait isShowWhy
Under 100 msNothingIt feels instant. Any indicator would be a flash.
100–300 msKeep the previous contentThe screen stays calm. A skeleton that flashes up and vanishes is worse than a short blank.
Over 300 ms, known shapeA skeleton (this page)It says "this is coming, here" and reserves the space.
Over 300 ms, unknown shape, or an actionA spinnerNothing to sketch: a save, a refresh, a button.
Over 2 s with a known lengthProgressA number beats an animation.
Over about 10 s, or failedAn empty state with RetryNever leave a skeleton on screen for ever.
Do

Mirror the real layout: the same number of rows, the same tile size, the same media ratio. Keep the heading and the controls real and sketch only what is loading.

Don't

Announce the skeleton, sweep a gradient across it, or show it for content that is already there. It is decoration, hidden from assistive technology.

Examples

Every piece sits in a .skeleton group that is aria-hidden and fades in after 300 ms. A shape is the ink mixed 26% into the surface under it, so it reads on the canvas, on a card and on a tone alike.

Text

Line widths are percentages, so a paragraph gets a natural ragged edge. The last line is shorter.

Tile, avatar and text

The tile is the size of .card__tile. The avatar is a circle, in three sizes. A line placed straight into a row is the short trailing value.

Card

The media block takes an aspect ratio (16/9 by default, 4/3, 1/1, 21/9), so its space is reserved before the image exists. That is what prevents layout shift. Inside a card the shapes mix with the card's own paper or tone.

List of rows

data-layout="list" pads each row to the height of a .card--row card (72px), so a list of placeholder rows turns into a list of real ones without moving anything. Below 18rem a card row drops its trail under the title and grows; the placeholder stays one row, which is the honest approximation.

The full pattern, live

The region that is loading carries aria-busy="true". When the content arrives: swap the skeleton for it, set aria-busy="false" and make one polite announcement. The buttons simulate a slow response (1.6 s) and a fast one (200 ms, so no skeleton ever appears).

Motion and timing

  • Delay: the group starts at opacity 0 and fades in after 300 ms (animation-delay), so there is no JavaScript timer to forget.
  • Stepped pulse: four held levels over 1.6 s (100%, 75%, 55%, 75% opacity), identical for every piece, so the screen breathes together. Nothing slides, nothing sweeps, nothing flashes: 2.5 small steps a second, below the 3-per-second line of WCAG 2.3.1.
  • Reduced motion: the pulse is off (animation: none); the shapes are still. The 300 ms fade-in stays: it is opacity only.
  • Never for ever: if the data has not arrived after about ten seconds, replace the skeleton with a message and a Retry button (see Empty state). Motion that cannot end fails WCAG 2.2.2 in spirit.

Anatomy

  1. Region: the real section, list or card that is loading, with aria-busy="true". Its heading and any controls stay real.
  2. Group .skeleton: aria-hidden="true"; owns the 300 ms delay and the gap between pieces.
  3. Pieces: __line (text, --radius-ctl), __tile (--radius-tile), __avatar and __pill (always round), __block (media, with an aspect ratio, --radius-tile). Square and soft corners follow the roles with no branch.
  4. Layout helpers: __row (a slot beside text), __stack (lines).

Accessibility

Skeleton screen reader and motion behaviour
InteractionBehaviour
Screen readerThe skeleton is invisible to it (aria-hidden). The region's aria-busy="true" says content is updating, and some readers hold back until it is false. When the content lands, one polite SG.announce("Recent sessions loaded") (WCAG 4.1.3). Do not announce "loading" on entry: it is noise.
KeyboardNothing in a skeleton is focusable. Do not sketch controls; keep the real ones.
Reduced motionStill. A skeleton is told from content by shape alone.
FlashingA slow, shallow, stepped dip, well under three a second (WCAG 2.3.1).
Forced coloursThe shapes are painted in GrayText; backgrounds would otherwise be removed and leave a blank region.
ContrastDecorative, so there is no contrast requirement (WCAG 1.4.11 covers meaningful graphics). The shapes are still visible against their surface.
