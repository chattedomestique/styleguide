Player

A player puts Play, Back, Forward, a seek bar, the time and Mute under a recording. The recording is a native <audio> or <video>; the browser decodes it, buffers it and keeps its own controls, and the script only swaps those for ones that match the rest of the app. If the script never loads, the browser's controls are all you see, and they work.

When to use

Do

Use it for a recording the person listens to or watches from start to finish: a voice memo, a lesson, a clip. Write what is in it (.player__desc, a transcript, or captions) so nobody has to press play to find out.

Don't

Autoplay, or start sound on load. Do not put controls over the picture (they would need a scrim to be readable on every frame), and do not rename Play to Pause when it toggles: the name stays, the state is aria-pressed.

Anatomy

  1. Frame (.player, role="group" with a label): a 2px rectangle. data-skip sets the jump.
  2. Stage (.player__stage, video only): the picture at 16 / 9 (--ratio changes it), object-fit: cover.
  3. Head (.player__head): title and a sentence about what is in it. The description is the media element's aria-describedby.
  4. Media (.player__media): the native element. Keeps controls until the script takes over.
  5. Seek bar: a Scrubber (data-variant="plain", data-format="time"). Its label is visually hidden.
  6. Transport (.player__transport, inside .player__row): Play, Back and Forward as one group. When the row is too narrow for them and Mute, Mute (.player__end) drops under the group, so the three never come apart. Under 18rem wide the circle goes from 56 to 44px and the pills tighten.
  7. Play (.player__play): the large primary circle. Two icons, one shows.
  8. Back and Forward (data-player="back|forward"): small pills with the arrow and the number of seconds they jump (data-skip). The digit is visible and is in the name ("Back 5 seconds"), so the label matches.
  9. Time (.player__time): position and length, tabular, aria-hidden (the seek bar says it).
  10. Captions and Mute (data-player="captions|mute"): toggles at the end of the row.
  11. Status (.player__status, role="status"): empty unless something needs saying.

Accessibility

Player keyboard and screen reader behaviour
InteractionBehaviour
TabSeek bar, Play, Back, Forward, Captions, Mute, in the order they read. Every control is a native button or range.
Play: Enter / SpacePlays or pauses. The name stays "Play"; a screen reader hears "Play, toggle button, pressed" while it plays. Finishing is announced.
Seek: Left Right PageUp PageDown Home EndNative range keys, one second per step. The value is spoken as "0:06 of 0:12". Clicking the bar jumps there.
Back, ForwardJump data-skip seconds and announce the new position, since focus stays on the button.
Screen readerThe group is named by the title; the media element is described by the sentence under it. Icons are hidden; every button has a name.
Captions and transcriptsVideo with speech needs a caption track (WCAG 1.2.2). Audio-only needs a transcript or an equivalent sentence (1.2.1). The Captions button appears only when there is a track.
TouchEvery target is 44px or has a 44px hit area. Nothing needs a drag: the bar jumps on tap, and the buttons step.
Autoplay and soundNever autoplays. Starting one player pauses the others.
No scriptThe browser's native controls remain and work.
Reduced motionNothing in the Player moves; the Button fills stay.
Forced coloursFrame and stage take CanvasText; buttons keep their borders; pressed is a 4px frame.
