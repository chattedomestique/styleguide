Space

Everything is laid out on a 4px grid. The space between the blocks of a layout is written in rem, so it grows with the reader's text size. The insets of controls and containers stop at their 100% size, so large text gets the room. Targets never shrink: every pressable thing has a 44px hit area, however small it is drawn.

The spacing scale

--space-0 to --space-9. One step is one or two grid units; the scale is tight at the bottom, where small differences matter, and opens up at the top, where they do not. The last column is the computed size in this browser.

  • Steps 6 to 9 are for page rhythm. Inside a component, steps 1 to 5 do everything. The layout primitives stop at data-gap="7".
  • Between blocks it is rem. The space between a title and its text, between cards and between sections grows with the reader's text size, which is what keeps large text from cramping (WCAG 1.4.4). The inset of a control or a container is chrome and stops at its 100% size: see Chrome caps. Line weights are the other exception: they stay in px on purpose (see Shape).
  • Logical properties. margin-inline, padding-block, inset-inline-start: the scale works unchanged in right-to-left scripts.

Chrome caps

Words grow with the reader's text size. The chrome around them does not grow past its 100% size. iOS and Android work the same way: Dynamic Type and Android font scale grow the words, not the icons, the padding or the tab bar. If the chrome grew with the text, 200% text on a phone would spend the width on frames: two 88px circles beside a title leave the title a few letters a line, and a button's 48px of side padding pushes its own label onto two lines.

Grows with the text (rem)

  • Words, and a control's height, so its label fits.
  • An icon that sits inside a line of words: the icon beside a button's label, a chip's check mark, the icon before a status message. It tracks its label.
  • The space between the blocks of a layout: --space-*, the gaps of .stack and .cluster, the room above a heading.

Stops at its 100% size (--chrome-*)

  • The insets of controls and containers: their padding, and the gaps inside a control.
  • Icon-only controls and their 44px targets.
  • The slots that hold them: a row's chevron or trailing icon, a bar control, a leading picture, avatar or tile.
  • Badges on icons, and the frames of pictures (avatars, swatches).
  • The gutter of the app column.

A cap is the smaller of the rem size and its size at 100% text: --chrome-4 is min(var(--space-4), 16px). At 100% text it is exactly the size it replaces, so nothing changes; it differs only when the text is larger. Browser zoom still scales everything, chrome included, because zoom scales CSS pixels. Set the text to 200% and compare the last column of the two tables: the spacing scale doubles, the caps do not.

  • Words or chrome? Ask what the size is for. If it holds or separates words, it is rem. If it is a frame, an inset, a target or a picture, it is chrome.
  • An icon beside words grows; an icon alone does not. The icon in "Add funds" is part of the line and grows with it. Beside a label that is not body size, size it in em (for example --ic-size: 1.15em) so it keeps its size next to the words: a capped 20px icon beside 32px words looks like a speck. Only the padding, the gaps and the target around it stop. The icon in a circle button is the whole control, so it is chrome (--chrome-ic-sm in a --chrome-ctl circle).
  • A cap never makes a target smaller than 44px. The caps stop growth; --chrome-hit is 44px at every text size.

Gutter and measure

--gutter is the gap between cards and the side padding of the app column. It runs from 16px on a phone to 28px on a desk, and it is chrome at both ends (the formula is in the table above), so a phone at 200% text keeps its width for the words. --measure is 62ch, the widest a paragraph gets. .card-grid spaces cards with the gutter; .card-grid--tiled closes it to zero and lets neighbouring frames share one 2px line.

Target sizes

--hit is 2.75rem (44px): the size every pressable thing must be, whatever it looks like. The drawn thing may be smaller; the target is not. Three control heights cover everything:

Control sizes
TokenDrawnHit areaUse
--ctl-sm36px (2.25rem)44pxDense rows and toolbars. Drawn smaller, tappable at full size.
--ctl44px (= --hit)44pxThe default.
--ctl-lg56px (3.5rem)56pxThe one primary action on a screen.
--ic-size20px (1.25rem)n/aThe default icon. Icons are never the target; the control around them is.

A control with a label grows with its text, so these heights are rem and they are minimums. A control that holds only an icon is chrome: the same three sizes as --chrome-ctl-sm, --chrome-ctl and --chrome-ctl-lg, with a --chrome-hit target, at every text size.

Targets are also spaced: at least 8px (--space-2) between two of them, so a thumb lands on one. That covers WCAG 2.5.8 (24px minimum) with room to spare, and meets the 44px of 2.5.5. Inline links inside a sentence are exempt; everything else is measured by npm run test:a11y.

Extending a hit area

A control drawn at 36px or 20px still gets a 44px target through an invisible ::after that grows to --hit when the box is smaller, and never shrinks it when the box is bigger. .btn carries it already. For anything else, add .hit-area (it sets position: relative and the ::after). The dashed outlines below are the real hit areas, drawn for this page only.

.hit-area { position: relative; }
.hit-area::after {
  content: "";
  position: absolute;
  inset-block: min(0px, calc((100% - var(--hit)) / 2));
  inset-inline: min(0px, calc((100% - var(--hit)) / 2));
}

Targets never shrink for density

There is no compact mode. Tightening a layout means less padding around controls, never smaller controls. A 20px control with .hit-area is for a control that is the only thing in its row, or has room on both sides: two of them side by side would have overlapping 44px areas. If two tiny controls must sit together, make them one 44px control, or space them so their areas are 8px apart.

Layers

In-flow stacking uses four named steps. Dialogs, popovers and toasts use the browser's top layer (<dialog>, popover), which sits above every z-index, so they need none.

The z-index layers
TokenValueFor
--z-raised10Something that must sit over its siblings inside a component.
--z-sticky20App bars. .sticky-top uses it.
--z-dock30Bottom navigation, above the app bar.
--z-overlay40In-flow overlays that cannot use the top layer: the skip link, a drawer and its scrim.

Sticky chrome also tells the page how tall it is, through --appbar-h and --dock-h (both 0px until set), and html uses them for scroll-padding-block, so a focused control is never hidden behind a bar (WCAG 2.4.11). The notch and home indicator are handled by the safe-area tokens: see Layout.

Do and don't

Do

Use a step: a --space-* step between blocks, a --chrome-* step inside a control or a frame. Use the layout primitives (.stack, .cluster, data-gap) before writing a margin. Give every small control a 44px hit area.

Don't

Write margin: 10px, use px for a gap between blocks, size an icon-only control in rem, shrink a target to fit a row, or put two targets closer than 8px.
