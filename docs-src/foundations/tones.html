Tones

A tone gives one element a fill and four text and accent colours chosen from a single hue, with contrast fixed by lightness rather than by the hue. Put data-tone on a card and it changes colour without changing how legible it is. Tones carry rhythm: they tell neighbours apart. They never carry meaning.

Using a tone

Set data-tone on the element that paints. Six numbered slots stand in for the palette; ink is the inverted tone; ok, warn, bad and info are the status tones. Which hue slot 3 is depends on the palette: open Appearance and change it, and every card below follows.

What a tone exposes

Every data-tone sets five roles on that element, and a component decides what to do with them. Two families of direct tokens sit beside them.

Roles a tone exposes
RoleWhat it isFloor
--tone-bgThe fill. A pastel by default; a saturated mid-dark with data-emphasis="bold"; the ink for data-tone="ink".
--tone-inkText and icons on the fill.7:1 (6.5:1 bold)
--tone-ink-softSecondary text on the fill. A mix of the ink and the fill (72% ink), so it stays on-hue.4.5:1
--tone-fillA solid pill or bar that sits on the tone: a button, a meter, a tag. Darker than the fill in light, lighter in dark.3:1 against the fill
--tone-on-fillText and icons drawn on --tone-fill.4.5:1
--tone-1 to --tone-6, --on-tone-1 to --on-tone-6The owner's direct slot tokens: the pastel fill and its ink for a slot, readable anywhere (they are plain, inherited colours). Use them for a swatch or a tile that is not itself toned. .card__media falls back to --tone-1.7:1
--tone-h-1 to --tone-h-6, --tone-c-1 to --tone-c-6The hue and vividness of each slot. A palette sets these; nothing else should.

    Why they are read on the element itself

    --tone-bg, --tone-ink, --tone-ink-soft, --tone-fill, --tone-on-fill and --tone-line are registered with @property … inherits: false in 00-foundation.css. A tone belongs to the element it is set on and to nothing inside it. Without that, a toned card would paint every toned-less child the same colour: a nested panel, a tile, a button would all turn the card's hue.

    The parent has data-tone="2". --tone-bg is checking here.

    A child with no tone. --tone-bg is checking here, so it falls back to whatever the component chose.

    What follows for you:

    • Read a tone on the toned element. A component that wants a tone colour reads it with a fallback, on the element that has data-tone: --card-bg: var(--tone-bg, var(--paper)).
    • Pass it down by copying. A custom property holding var(--tone-bg) is resolved where it is declared and then inherits as a normal value, so a component copies what its children need into a private property (--card-ink-soft, --_fill) on the toned element. The specimens on this page do exactly that.
    • A toned thing inside a toned thing needs its own data-tone. A tone button inside a tone card is <button class="btn" data-variant="tone" data-tone="3">: the button reads its own tone, not the card's.
    • --tone-line is registered but no tone sets it yet. Frames use --line (see Inside a toned card).

    The switch itself is a few lines in 20-tones.css: the attribute sets a hue and vividness and the roles are derived from them.

    [data-tone="1"] { --th: var(--tone-h-1); --tc: var(--tone-c-1); --tone-bg: var(--tone-1); --tone-ink: var(--on-tone-1); }
[data-tone="ok"] { --th: var(--hue-ok); --tc: 0.15; }

[data-tone]:not([data-tone="ink"]) {
  --tone-ink-soft: color-mix(in srgb, var(--tone-ink) 72%, var(--tone-bg));
  --tone-fill: light-dark(oklch(var(--k-tone-fill-l) calc(var(--tc) * var(--k-tone-fill-c)) var(--th)), …);
  --tone-on-fill: light-dark(oklch(var(--k-tone-on-fill-l) calc(var(--tc) * 0.08) var(--th)), …);
}

    Why they always pass

    A tone's contrast is decided by lightness before any hue is chosen, exactly as in Colour. The knobs are plain numbers in 20-tones.css:

    Tone lightness knobs
    ToneFill (L)Ink (L)Why
    Pastel, light--k-tone-l 0.93 (0.96 higher contrast)--k-tone-ink-l 0.3 (0.2)A very light fill with dark ink is 7:1 or more whatever the hue and chroma (the header of 20-tones.css records it as measured over the sRGB gamut), so a palette cannot break it. The test checks the real palettes.
    Pastel, dark--k-tone-d 0.27 (0.2)--k-tone-ink-d 0.95 (1)The mirror of light.
    Bold--k-tone-b-l 0.44 (0.38)--k-tone-b-ink 0.985A saturated mid-dark fill with near-white ink. The same in light and dark. At least 6.5:1.
    Fill pill--k-tone-fill-l 0.34 (0.24), --k-tone-fill-d 0.82 (0.9)--k-tone-on-fill-l 0.97, --k-tone-on-fill-d 0.2A pill that is clearly darker (light) or lighter (dark) than the fill, with its own label colour.

    The wire palette steps the six pastel fills down from 0.94 to 0.8 (light) so slots are told apart by value alone, and darkens the ink to 0.2 to keep the gap.

    Bold

    Card's soft ink on a bold tone

    card.css derives --card-ink-soft as a 72% mix of ink and fill. That equals --tone-ink-soft on a pastel, but on a bold tone it measures about 4.2:1, under the 4.5:1 floor, where --tone-ink-soft measures 5.7:1. Until Card reads the tone's own value, add .card[data-emphasis="bold"] { --card-ink-soft: var(--tone-ink-soft); } to your app CSS. The specimens on this page already do.

    The ink tone has no bold form: it is already the strongest fill there is.

    The ink tone

    data-tone="ink" is the inverted tone: an ink fill with paper text, and a paper pill (--tone-fill: var(--paper), --tone-on-fill: var(--ink)). It is the one tone that does not depend on the palette. A card swaps its bar for paper on this tone, and its focus ring follows the surface, because an ink ring on an ink card would vanish.

    Status tones

    data-tone="ok", "warn", "bad" and "info" run the same formulas as the six slots, with the hue from --hue-ok (155), --hue-warn (80), --hue-bad (18) and --hue-info (240). They get the same guarantees. They are not enough on their own: a status always needs an icon or words as well as the colour (WCAG 1.4.1), and in the wire palette their chroma is 0, so they turn grey and the icon and words are all that is left.

    For a line of status text on a plain surface, use the ink roles --ok-ink, --warn-ink, --bad-ink and --info-ink (see Colour). They are tested on the four surfaces; tones are for whole cards.

    Inside a toned card

    Everything you put in a toned card keeps its contrast if it reads the tone's roles, and the defaults already do.

    • Text and icons inherit --tone-ink; .ic masks take currentColor. Secondary text is --tone-ink-soft, which has a 4.5:1 floor, not 7:1: do not put small meta text on it.
    • Links are underlined (2px) and take the card's ink. No hue is needed to find them.
    • Buttons. A plain .btn has a paper fill and an ink frame, so it reads on any tone. A tone button needs its own data-tone and reads --tone-bg at rest and --tone-fill on hover.
    • Frames and rules use --line on a pastel tone (at least 3:1 is tested). On a bold or ink tone --line is not tested against the fill, so draw lines and icons there with --tone-ink. The card's bar and focus ring already do.

It is the squint test for tones: if a screen only works because slot 2 is blue and slot 3 is coral, colour was carrying meaning.

    Do and don't

    Do

    Use tones for rhythm: alternate slots down a list of decks so neighbours are told apart, and use data-emphasis="bold" for the one card that should win.

    Don't

    Let a tone say something. Slot 3 is coral in one palette and green in another, so "the red card means overdue" breaks the moment the palette changes. Say it with data-tone="bad" and an icon and words.

    Add a tone

    • A different hue for a slot: in a palette block, set --tone-h-N and --tone-c-N (hue and vividness). Contrast cannot change, because it does not come from them.
    • A new status tone: add a --hue-* in 20-tones.css, a selector that sets --th and --tc and includes it in the status :is() list, and add its name to TONES in tests/contrast.mjs. Run npm run test:contrast.
    • A seventh slot: don't. Six is the owner's number, and a palette can only tell six apart.
