Top bar

A screen's title, its way back, and one or two actions. It runs edge to edge across the top of the frame and starts under the status bar.

01

Variants

Three parts: the back link, the title, up to two actions. The back link and the actions are Buttons, so they press, lift and ring like every other button. Bars here are 390px wide, a phone's width; the titles are h4 because this page has its own headings, and an h1 in an app.

1.1

Standard

Any screen past the root.

.topbar .topbar__back .topbar__title .topbar__actions

The back link names the screen it goes to ("Decks"), or says "Back" when there isn't one. The title is centred between the two sides; when room runs short, the back link's label gives way first. Actions are quiet Buttons, text or icon-only, padded 12px a side in a bar.

1.2

Root

A place at the root that still wants a bar for its actions.

.topbar--root

No way back, so the title leads, lined up with the content's gutter. A root screen with nothing to put in a bar has no bar at all (Frame's root screen).

1.3

With a large heading

A long title, or a screen that opens with its name in big type.

.topbar without .topbar__title, then .t-heading

A recipe, not a class. The bar keeps the back link and the actions, and the screen's heading is the first thing in the content, at --type-heading, where it can wrap. It doesn't shrink into the bar as you scroll: that's motion, and gate 4's.

Numbers to twenty

Large text. Under 16em, a phone at about 150% text, the title takes its own row and wraps instead of truncating to nothing. The bar grows down, never sideways. And "Back", for a screen opened from a link, with nowhere named to go back to.

The .is-* classes force a state for this page and for tests. They are not API: real states come from :hover (only where a pointer hovers), :focus-visible and :active.

03

Usage

  1. Do

    Name the place the back link goes ("Decks"), and keep titles to what's on the deck's own card. Put the action people come back for in the bar; everything else goes in a menu.

  2. Don't

    Don't make the way back a button that calls history.back(): it's a link to a place, so it can be opened, copied and read out. Don't add a third action, a primary button, or a search field to the bar.

  3. Installed PWA

    Standalone has no browser back, so every screen past the root has this bar with a back link. It owns the status bar's inset (--safe-top), and its paper runs up behind it.

  4. Mac and SwiftUI

    Not on a Mac: there the toolbar is the system's. In SwiftUI it's .navigationTitle(_:) with .navigationBarTitleDisplayMode(.inline) and .toolbar items; the back button is the system's, named after the previous screen.
