Sheet

A task on top of a screen: from the bottom on a phone, from the side on a wide screen, with a close button you can always see.

01

Anatomy

A modal dialog shaped as a panel: the top bar’s layout, a body that scrolls, and the bottom safe area. Drawn here over a deck, on a phone.

6
  1. 1
    Scrim::backdrop --scrim

    Near-black at 60% in both schemes, as behind a Dialog: the deck is still there, but not now, and inert. A tap on it closes an action sheet, never a form.

  2. 2
    Surface.sheet <dialog>

    A container, so square: paper, a 2px frame on the edge that faces the page, no shadow. Edge to edge along the bottom, as tall as the task up to 90% of the screen. From 45rem of window it comes from the side instead.

  3. 3
    Top bar.topbar

    Top bar’s standard layout: the task as the title, in an h2 that names the sheet, centred. It stays put while the body scrolls. A bottom sheet stops short of the status bar, so the bar pads no safe area.

  4. 4
    Close.btn--icon ic--close

    Button, quiet and icon-only, named “Close”, in a <form method="dialog"> that is the bar’s actions slot. Always visible: a bottom sheet can also be dragged down by its top bar, but the drag is never the only way out.

  5. 5
    Body.sheet__body

    The task: here Form, with its submit row last. It scrolls inside the sheet and keeps its scroll to itself; the page never moves, sideways or down.

  6. 6
    Bottom safe area--safe-bottom

    The sheet’s, as a bar’s: the body pads it, plus 8px, so the last button clears the home indicator.

02

Variants

Three, each drawn and live. The button opens the real modal, which moves in from its own edge while the scrim fades: focus moves in at once and stays in, Escape or Close shuts it, and focus goes back to the button. With Reduce Motion on, it fades where it stands.

2.1

Bottom sheet

A short task over the screen it belongs to, on a phone: add a card to this deck.

.sheet .topbar .sheet__body autofocus

The same markup is the side sheet once the window is 45rem wide, so on this screen the live one may come from the side: narrow the window to see it rise from the bottom. There, drag its top bar down to close it: past a third of its height, or with a flick, it goes as Close sends it; short of that, it settles back. Focus starts in Front (autofocus): the task is typing. No light dismiss, so a stray tap can’t throw away what you typed.

2.2

Side sheet

The same task on a wide screen, where the deck stays in view beside it.

.sheet from 45rem, or .sheet--side

Along the end edge, top to bottom, 28rem at most, framed on the side that faces the page. It runs from the top, so its top bar pads the top safe area. An open .sheet becomes one by itself; .sheet--side pins it whatever the width, as the live one here does.

2.3

Action sheet

A thing’s actions on a phone, in reach of a thumb: the phone’s Menu.

.sheet--action .menu__item .menu__rule closedby="any"

Menu’s items, order and rule, with the destructive item last; Menu’s no-anchor shape: edge to edge on a phone, 30rem and centred on a wider window. The body is a <form method="dialog">, so each item closes the sheet with its value. A tap on the scrim closes it too, as a click outside closes a menu.

04

Usage

  1. Do

    Use a sheet for a short task that belongs to the screen under it, such as adding a card to this deck. Title it with the task, keep Close in view, and end the form with its own button.

  2. Don’t

    Don’t open a sheet from a sheet, or make a drag the only way out. A question is a Dialog, news is a Toast or a Banner, and a task with screens of its own is a screen with a back link, not a sheet.

  3. Installed PWA

    From the bottom, edge to edge, owning the home indicator’s safe area; at most 90% of the screen, so the deck still shows above it. On a phone, a thing’s actions open as an action sheet; on a wide window, as a Menu. It rises in, and a drag down on its top bar closes it, as Close does; no detents, since a sheet is as tall as its task.

  4. Mac and SwiftUI

    Sheets are system chrome on the Mac: SwiftUI’s .sheet, with .inspector for a side panel and .confirmationDialog for an action sheet. This element is the phone’s and the web shell’s.
