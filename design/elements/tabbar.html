Tab bar

The three to five places at the root of a phone app. Each one is a link with an icon and a word, and the place you're in is marked the way a chosen card is: its frame doubles.

01

Variants

One bar, three to five links. The icon sits in a pill, the indicator: that is what fills, lifts and is marked. The target is the whole item, at least 44px each way.

The .is-* classes force a state for this page and for tests. They are not API: real states come from :hover (only where a pointer hovers), :focus-visible, :active and aria-current. The bars above are labelled by their state only so this page's landmarks have distinct names; an app has one, labelled "Places".

03

Usage

  1. Do

    Use it for places, not actions: each item goes somewhere and stays there. Keep the same items, in the same order, on every root screen, and one word each.

  2. Don't

    Don't drop the labels to save room, put an action ("Add") in the bar, or badge more than one place. Don't hide the bar on scroll: that's motion, and it moves the targets.

  3. Large text

    iOS keeps its tab bar one row high and shows a big copy of an item on a long press. The web has no such viewer, so the labels have to be readable where they are: ours grow with the text and take a second row when they must.

  4. Installed PWA

    It owns the home indicator's inset (--safe-bottom). While the on-screen keyboard is up it hides, so the field has the room: Frame's frame.js sees the keyboard as lost viewport height. Without script it stays.

  5. Mac and SwiftUI

    Not on a Mac: places there are the system sidebar. In SwiftUI it's a TabView with a .tabItem (a Label) per place and .badge(_:) for the count; the system draws it.
