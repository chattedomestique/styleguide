List

Rows of things, as wide as the screen: decks, settings, a deck's numbers. A row is a rectangle that holds; when it goes somewhere, the whole row is the link.

01

Anatomy

A heading, then rows. Each row has a title, and may have a lead, meta, a trail and actions. .card__list stays the card's; this is the screen's.

02

Variants

Two frames (plain and inset) and four kinds of row. The boxes here are a phone's width; the outline stands for the screen's edges.

2.1

Plain

Rows straight on the screen: a deck's numbers, search results.

.list .list__rows .list__row .list__main .list__title .list__trail

Edge to edge: straight in a Frame's scroll region it reaches over the region's side padding, and its rows take that padding, so their text lines up with the content above. Elsewhere its rows keep the screen's --gutter, and rules close it top and bottom, so its edges show without colour. A row that only shows a value is a <div> and has no states.

2.2

Inset

A group that belongs together: a deck's settings, one screen's few links.

.list--inset

Panel-style: a square 2px frame on paper, inside the screen's gutter, with 16px in the rows. A frame contains, so it's 2px; the rules inside only divide, so they're 1px.

2.3

With section headers

A long screen in a few named parts: decks by what's due, settings by topic.

.list__head

One .list per section, each with its heading inside; sections stack 24px apart. Plain, the heading is a label over the rule. Inset, it is the Panel's bar. Use real heading elements, in order, so the screen can be skimmed by heading.

2.4

Row as a link

Each row opens something: a deck, a card, a setting's own screen.

a.list__row .list__tile .list__meta ic--forward

The <a> is the row, as in the card's list rows: a tap anywhere lands, and it is one tab stop. Its name is everything in it, so the title goes first. The chevron says "goes somewhere"; a row that doesn't, doesn't get one.

2.5

Settings row

A setting that takes effect at once, among others.

.switch in a .list__rows item

The row is Switch's own markup, whole: its label is the row and the target, and help goes under it. The list only adds its padding and the rules.

2.6

Row with actions

Things you also rename, copy or delete: a list of decks.

.list__actions .btn--icon .menu

A More button after the row opens a Menu: Rename, Duplicate, Export, Delete deck…. Its name says whose actions they are ("More for Shapes"). If an app adds swipe (gate 4, or its own), the swipe offers these same actions and the button stays: without script, the button is all there is. The row keeps its link; the chevron goes, so the row doesn't end in two marks.

03

States

Only a link row has states. No lift: a shadow would fall across the next row. Hover underlines the title; press fills the row with ink. A settings row's states are Switch's; the More button's are Button's.

The .is-* classes force a state for this page and for tests. They are not API: real states come from :hover (only where a pointer hovers), :focus-visible and :active.

04

Usage

  1. Do

    Make the whole row the link when it goes somewhere, title first. Put every action a row has behind a visible More button, and name it after the row ("More for Shapes").

  2. Don't

    Don't make swipe the only way to an action, and don't put a chevron on a row that doesn't go anywhere. Don't repeat one glyph down every row: a lead says something or it goes.

  3. Installed PWA

    A plain list runs under the screen's gutter to both edges; the frame, not the list, keeps clear of the notch. Press shows on touch with the shell's touchstart listener. Swipe, if an app adds it, waits for gate 4.

  4. Mac and SwiftUI

    A List with our row as a custom view; on the Mac a selectable collection is a List or Table, so ⌘ and ⇧ selection come free, and the More menu is also a context menu and a menu bar item.
