Toast

It's done, with Undo. One line about what just happened, above the tab bar for a few seconds, and never the only way to act on it.

01

Anatomy

A region that is always there, and in it, one at a time, a framed line of words with at most one action and a way to send it off. Shown here in place, as one looks just after a deck was deleted.

  1. 1
    Region.toasts role="status"

    Always there, empty until it's needed: one per frame, after the scroll region. It shares the scroll region's grid cell and sits at its foot, so a toast is 12px above the tab bar whatever the bar's height. With no bottom bar it adds the home indicator's safe area; outside a frame it is fixed to the foot of the window. Polite: putting a toast in is what a screen reader announces. Taps pass through it.

  2. 2
    Surface.toast

    A container, so square: a 2px frame on paper and no shadow, since a shadow means "press this". It floats over the content, and the frame is what parts the two. At most 32rem wide; the content's width on a phone.

  3. 3
    Glyph.toast__icon

    ic--check when it's done, ic--warning when it failed. The palette is monochrome, so the glyph tells the two apart before you read.

  4. 4
    Words.toast__text

    What happened, in one short sentence: "Deck deleted." Body type, wrapped, never cut. The glyph and the first line centre on the buttons' 44px row.

  5. 5
    Action.toast__action

    At most one, a Button with a frame, 12px a side. A verb: Undo, Try again. Pressing it does it and closes the toast. Never the only way to do it.

  6. 6
    Dismiss.btn--quiet.btn--icon

    Button, quiet and icon-only, named "Dismiss", last in .toast__actions. On every toast, so it can go before its time and stop covering what's under it.

02

Variants

Three, told apart by their words, their glyph and whether they offer anything. All three are polite: none of them stops you doing what you were doing. The script builds this markup; the page prints it.

2.1

Done, with Undo

Something just happened that can be taken back.

.toast .toast__action ic--check

Ten seconds, for time to reach the action. Undo here is a shortcut, not the only way back: the app needs one that doesn't run out (on the Mac, Edit › Undo, ⌘Z).

Deck deleted.

2.2

Done

It worked, and there is nothing to do about it.

.toast ic--check

Six seconds, then it goes. Dismiss is its only button. If the words say nothing the screen doesn't already show, leave the toast out.

Saved on this device.

2.3

Failed, with a retry

Something in the background didn't work, and nothing is lost.

.toast .toast__action ic--warning

Polite all the same: a sync that failed doesn't stop you studying. Try again is also wherever sync lives in the app, and the app tries again by itself. A failure that does stop you is a Banner or a Dialog, not a toast.

03

States

The surface doesn't press or lift; its buttons are Button's. What changes is time: a toast waits while you're on it, and only one shows at a time. Forced here with .is-* on the buttons; live in the phone below.

Showing. It appears in place, with no motion, and is announced once, as it goes into the region. 10 s with an action, 6 s without.

Deck deleted.

Try it

The three buttons raise the three toasts in this phone's region.

data-toast Flashcards.toast()

Press two or three quickly: they queue and show one at a time. Rest the pointer on a toast or Tab into it, and it waits. ⌥T jumps to it from anywhere on the page; Escape closes it. Undo only closes it here; in the app it puts the deck back. Without script the buttons aren't shown, because they couldn't do anything.

Placement

No bottom bar

A screen past the root with no tab bar: the toast clears the home indicator itself.

.frame:not(:has(> .frame__bottom)) > .toasts

The region adds --safe-bottom under its 12px, so the toast sits above the home indicator, never on it. Content never reads the safe area; the region does, as a bar would.

04

Usage

  1. Do

    Say what happened in a few words, past tense, and offer the one thing worth doing about it. Raise it after the work is done, and after any dialog or sheet has closed: the page behind a modal is inert, and so is its region.

  2. Don't

    Don't make it the only way to undo or retry (WCAG 2.2.1), don't put a question or a form in it, don't use it for a failure that stops you, and don't stack two. No shadow, no round corners, no motion.

  3. Installed PWA

    Above the tab bar, clear of the home indicator when there is no bar. Its clock stops while the app is in the background, so a toast raised as you switch away is still there when you come back.

  4. Mac and SwiftUI

    The action is a command first: Edit › Undo with ⌘Z, in the menu bar. The toast mirrors it. In SwiftUI it is a custom overlay at the bottom of the window, announced with an accessibility announcement; there is no system toast.
