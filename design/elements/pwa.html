PWA

What turns the elements into an app on a home screen: it installs, works offline, updates when you say so, asks before it notifies, shares, and makes room for the keyboard. No new parts. Each pattern is made of elements you already have, and pwa.js switches it on where the browser can.

01

Anatomy

A few files and a little script. The screens are made of elements; the manifest tells the home screen what the app is; the service worker keeps it on the device; and pwa.js, in dist/flashcards.min.js, connects the screen to both.

1.1

The platform, working

A starter app to copy: one root screen, installable, and working offline.

design/pwa/

Copy it with dist/ and fonts/ to start an app. It installs and works offline only when it's served: run node design/tools/serve.mjs and open it on localhost, which counts as secure. Opened from disk it shows the screen, with no worker behind it.

What the browser and the home screen know about the app before it runs.

The two colours are --canvas: tools/bundle.mjs writes them in from the colour file, so the hex lives in one place and the splash, the status bar and the first frame are one ground. 1.3

The service worker

What sw.js keeps on the device, how it answers, and how it hands over to a new version.

sw.js <meta name="flashcards-sw">

pwa.js registers the worker the meta names, and only then: a page without the meta, like this one, never gets a worker. tools/bundle.mjs stamps the worker's version from the shell files' hash, so changing any of them makes a new worker, and changing none doesn't.

  • The shellThe starter's pages, flashcards.min.css, flashcards.min.js, the font and the icons. Cached when the worker installs, under its version, and served from there first: it opens at once, with or without a connection.
  • Anything else on the same originStale while revalidate: the cached copy now, a fresh one saved for next time. Fast, and never more than one visit out of date.
  • A page never cached, offlineoffline.html (2.3), never the browser's own error page.
  • Anything on another originNot touched: no respondWith, straight to the network. The worker never stands between the app and someone else's server.
  1. A new worker waits

    It installs beside the old one, which keeps running the open app.

  2. The band says so

    The update Banner shows: "A new version is ready." with Reload (2.2).

  3. Reload hands over

    It posts SKIP_WAITING to the waiting worker, which takes over.

  4. One reload

    On controllerchange the page reloads, once. Never on its own: you might be halfway through a card.

1.4

The hooks

What pwa.js looks for in the markup, and what it does there.

js/components/pwa.js data-pwa data-share

Everything is detected by feature, never by browser name, and switched on by markup. Anything it drives ships hidden, so without script, or where the browser can't, nothing shows that wouldn't work.

2.7

Keyboard

Make room for the on-screen keyboard, and keep the field you're typing in above it.

interactive-widget=resizes-content data-keyboard .frame

Every page's viewport meta adds interactive-widget=resizes-content, so Android shrinks the page above the keyboard instead of covering it. A focused field scrolls clear with Frame's scroll padding. While the keyboard is up the Tab bar hides, where the browser shows it: frame.js sees the keyboard as lost viewport height (visualViewport) and sets data-keyboard on the frame. Elsewhere the bar stays.

An installed app has no browser around it: no back, no address, no refresh.

2.11

Performance budget

What a served app downloads the first time, and the most it may.

dist/flashcards.min.css dist/flashcards.min.js tools/bundle.mjs

tools/bundle.mjs writes flashcards.min.css and flashcards.min.js (comments and spare whitespace out, nothing else), which a served app links, prints the gzipped sizes and fails the build over budget. First load on a mid-range phone stays under 2.5s to the largest paint, a target measured on a device; after that, the worker makes every load instant.

CSS, gzipped 13.1 of 30 KB
Script, gzipped 6.2 of 10 KB
The font 88 of 90 KB

Measured on 2026-10-20 with this wave's script in. The commented flashcards.js is 9.9 KB gzipped, so the budget is the min file's. The font is already compressed, so its file size is the number; preload it in a served app, with crossorigin. A KB is 1024 bytes. tools/bundle.mjs prints these on every build.

03

States

What the app shows, what a screen reader hears, and where focus is, as the platform changes under it. Nothing moves on screen until gate 4: each appears in place.

  1. Offline

    Shows: The offline band first in the scroll region; everything on the device under it.

    Heard: "You're offline. Changes are saved on this device.", politely, once.

    Focus: Where it was.

  2. Online again

    Shows: The band goes.

    Heard: Nothing.

    Focus: Where it was.

  3. Update waiting

    Shows: The update band, with Reload.

    Heard: "A new version is ready.", politely, once.

    Focus: Where it was; Reload is the first stop in the content.

  4. Reloaded

    Shows: The same screen, on the new version, with the band gone.

    Heard: The page, from the top, as after any reload.

    Focus: At the top.

  5. Installable

    Shows: Install in Settings, beside the version. Nowhere else.

    Heard: Nothing until you reach it.

    Focus: Where it was.

  6. Installing

    Shows: Chromium's own install sheet; on iOS, the Sheet with the steps.

    Heard: The browser's sheet, or the Sheet's title.

    Focus: In the sheet; back on Install when it closes.

  7. Not asked yet

    Shows: After a session, the reminder card with Not now and Turn on.

    Heard: Nothing: it's there when you look.

    Focus: Where it was; the card's buttons come next.

  8. Asked

    Shows: The browser's prompt. Then the card goes; if the answer was no, the setting says how to allow it.

    Heard: The browser's prompt.

    Focus: Back on the page, at the scroll region, since the card has gone.

  9. Link copied

    Shows: Toast's done, "Link copied.", for 6 seconds.

    Heard: "Link copied.", politely.

    Focus: On Share.

  10. Keyboard up

    Shows: The Tab bar goes; the field you're in stays above the keyboard.

    Heard: Nothing new: the bar is out of the way, not announced.

    Focus: In the field.

  11. Keyboard down

    Shows: The Tab bar is back.

    Heard: Nothing.

    Focus: Where it was.

  12. Imported

    Shows: A Toast: "Added 2 decks from the file." The decks are there.

    Heard: The Toast, politely.

    Focus: On Import.

At 200% text each pattern wraps and grows taller, never wider. In forced colours the bands' rules, the cards' and toasts' frames, the switch's knob and every glyph stay, in system colours.

04

Usage

  1. Do

    Ask for things after the person did something that needs them: notifications after a session, keeping the data after real use. Keep every count and setting inside the app too, and keep Install in Settings.

  2. Don't

    Don't prompt on load, reload without asking, lock the orientation, or show Install to an installed app. Don't badge what the app doesn't also show, or let the worker touch another origin's requests.

  3. Installed PWA

    Install, web push and the keyboard behave differently on iOS, and only a device shows how: an iPhone installs through Add to Home Screen, gets push only once installed, and lays the keyboard over the page. Each is marked as needing a device in the spec.

  4. Mac and SwiftUI

    Installed from Chrome or Safari (File, Add to Dock), the app gets its own window and Dock icon, and the badge goes on the Dock icon. Natively these are the system's: the App Store installs, UNUserNotificationCenter asks, ShareLink shares.
