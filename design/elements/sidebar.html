Sidebar

The places, on a wide screen: a column of links beside the content. When the screen narrows it folds away and the tab bar takes over, with the same links.

01

Variants

A head with the toggle, a body of links, an optional foot. Each link is a quiet Button that goes somewhere (<a class="btn btn--quiet">), so it presses, lifts and rings like one. This is the web shell's sidebar; on a Mac the system draws its own.

With sections

Places, then the things in them: the decks.

.sidebar__heading .sidebar__foot

A section heading is a label, in uppercase, and names its list. Decks have no icon: the same glyph on every row would be decoration, so their labels line up with the others instead. The foot holds what the app puts there, after a 1px rule.

03

Usage

  1. Do

    Give it the same places as the tab bar, in the same order, so folding changes where they are and not what they are. Mark here on the deepest place that matches the screen.

  2. Don't

    Don't mark here with a fill alone, which reads as pressed. Don't put actions in the list: the foot can hold one, as a Button. Don't slide it in or out: folding is layout, so it folds in place, and only its links' lift and fill ease.

  3. Installed PWA

    On a tablet it owns the safe areas it touches. On a phone it never shows: the frame is under 45rem, so the tab bar has the places. The toggle's state is the app's, kept between visits.

  4. Mac and SwiftUI

    On a Mac the sidebar is the system's (Screens shows it drawn), with View › Hide Sidebar and ⌃⌘S. In SwiftUI it is NavigationSplitView with a List(selection:) of NavigationLinks and Section headers; the system draws it, in its own font and colours.
