Segments

One of two to four views or modes, side by side. A radio group drawn as joined pills: it changes how one thing is shown, and never takes you anywhere.

01

Anatomy

A fieldset of native radios. Each option is a label: the radio, stretched over it and drawn as nothing, and the segment you see. The legend and help are Field's, in Field's order.

  1. 1
    Legend.segmented__legend

    Names the choice, in Field's label style: 16px, weight 600, sentence case. It may be visually hidden with .sr-only where the options name themselves (Grid, List), but it is always there.

  2. 2
    Help.field__help

    Field's help, optional, tied to the fieldset with aria-describedby.

  3. 3
    Track.segmented__track

    The row: equal columns, as wide as the widest option. It draws nothing itself except the focus ring, which goes round the whole pill.

  4. 4
    Option.segmented__option .segmented__input

    A <label>: the target, 44px tall, frame included. Inside it a native type="radio", stretched over the option with appearance: none, so it is what you click and what the arrows move.

  5. 5
    Segment.segmented__label

    What you see: 16px words at weight 600, a 2px frame, round at the two ends. Neighbours share one divider. Chosen, it fills with ink.

  6. 6
    Icon.ic

    Optional, before the words, 20px and aria-hidden. Never instead of them.

02

Variants

Three, from the same parts. Two to four options, one word each, one always chosen. When they can't sit side by side, they come apart into a stack of pills.

2.1

Text only

The default: two to four words.

.segmented .segmented__legend .segmented__track .segmented__option .segmented__input .segmented__label

Every radio in a group shares one name, and one is checked from the start. Above a grid of decks, Grid and List say what they are, so the legend is there for screen readers only (.sr-only).

2.2

Icons and text

When a glyph helps you find the option faster.

.ic in .segmented__label

The glyph leads the word and never replaces it: aria-hidden, 20px. An icon costs about as much room as another option, so this variant comes apart sooner.

2.3

Full width

On a phone: the options share the whole row.

.segmented--full

Each option gets an equal share. Below five and a half ems an option (seven and a quarter with an icon) the pills come apart: one per row, the whole width, 8px between them. Ems, so it follows the text size: four options come apart on a 320px phone, and most controls do at 200% text.

03

States

Chosen is the fill, the one state that's always on show. A segment never lifts: joined, a shadow would fall across its neighbours. Choosing crossfades two fills; nothing slides between them. There is no invalid state, because one option is always chosen.

04

Usage

  1. Do

    Use it for how one thing is shown: Grid or List, which side of the card, which cards. Two to four options, one short word each, and the change happens at once.

  2. Don't

    Don't use it to go somewhere: sections of a screen are Tabs, places are the tab bar. Five options or more, or long words, are a Select or a Radio group.

  3. Installed PWA

    Touch has no hover, so the press fills the segment straight away. Full width on a phone; at large text the pills come apart rather than squeeze.

  4. Mac and SwiftUI

    In the toolbar it's the system's own segmented control; ours is for content. In SwiftUI, a Picker with .pickerStyle(.segmented).
