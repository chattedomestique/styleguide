Buttons

A button starts an action. It is a pill because it acts, it is always 44px tall, and its words say what will happen.

01

Variants

One size, one shape, three levels of emphasis. Every variant is a real <button> with .btn; the modifiers only change what it looks like at rest.

1.1

Primary

The one thing to press in this part of the screen.

.btn .btn--primary

Filled with --accent and --on-accent (ink and paper until an app sets its own). At most one per region: if two things both want to be primary, one of them isn't.

Two regions, one primary each.

1.2

Secondary

Everything else that deserves a frame.

.btn

The default: .btn with no modifier is a 2px ink frame on paper. A leading icon is optional and comes before the label.

1.3

Quiet

For bars and card feet, where a frame would be noise.

.btn .btn--quiet

No frame until hover or focus, but the full 44px target and the same padding, so the label sits where a framed one would. Next to a primary it is the way out.

1.4

Destructive

Something that can't be taken back.

.btn .btn--quiet

A recipe, not a class. The words carry it: a verb and the thing it destroys. Never primary, never red (the palette is monochrome, for good). It always asks first, as here, or offers Undo afterwards.

First press. Opens the question.

Delete Shapes?

Its 12 cards and what you've learned go with it.

The question. The way out is quiet, the loss is named.

1.5

Icon-only

When the glyph says it all, and space is short.

.btn--icon aria-label

A 44px circle. The button carries the aria-label; the icon is aria-hidden. Works with any emphasis: framed by default, quiet in a bar.

1.6

Toggle

An action that stays on until you turn it off.

aria-pressed

The label never changes; aria-pressed says on or off. On doubles the frame, as a selected card does, and shows a check, so it can't be mistaken for focus or for a primary. Press one to try it.

1.7

Block

The full width of a phone form.

.btn--block

Takes the width of its column, nothing more. On a wide screen the column is narrow, so the button is too.

1.8

Busy

The press was heard, and the work takes a moment.

aria-busy .spinner

The label stays; there is no "Loading…" text. A spinner (from Meter) takes the leading icon's place, it stops lifting, and the app ignores another press until the work is done.

Before.

Busy. Same label, same width.

1.9

A row of buttons

Two or three choices side by side.

.btn-row

12px apart, so targets never touch. The primary goes last, at the trailing end, as on the Mac and iOS. Block buttons in a row share it equally and stack when each would be under three targets wide.

The study screen. Shared, equal widths.

A deck. Each as wide as its words.

02

States

The card's two numbers: --lift draws the hard shadow, --fill swaps ink and paper. Rest is flat, hover lifts, press goes back down and keeps the fill. Both numbers ease: 200ms in and out, 130ms as a press lands; the ring is instant. Under reduced motion the lift appears at once and the fill fades. Secondary, primary and quiet in each.

Rest. Flat. The pill already says it can be pressed.

Hover. Filled, with a 4px hard shadow. Nothing moves.

Focus. The hover look, plus a 3px ring 3px out.

Pressed. Back down: the shadow goes, the fill stays, and a 2px ring shows inside the frame, so even a primary answers a tap.

Selected. A toggle that's on: the frame doubles and a check shows. Here at rest and hovered.

Add a card to this deck first.

Disabled. Dashed and faint, still focusable, and it says why. Prefer not disabling at all.

The .is-* classes force a state for this page and for tests. They are not API: real states come from :hover (only where a pointer hovers), :focus-visible, :active, aria-pressed, aria-busy and aria-disabled.

03

Usage

A button reads the surface it sits on. In a card it takes the card's ink and fill, so the same markup works on paper, on a tone and on the inverted tone.

3.1

On a card

The same buttons on paper, a tone and ink.

--card-ink --card-bg

On the ink card the frame, the label and the shadow all turn paper. The card's own round action and square bar control stay the card's for now: the spec says how they line up.

On the tone and ink cards the second button is held in its hover look, to show the lift on each surface.

  1. Do

    Start the label with a verb and name the thing: "Add a shape", "Delete deck". Keep it to two or three words. One primary per region, last in the row.

  2. Don't

    Don't disable a button to say "not yet": leave it on and explain when it's pressed. Don't make a link look like a button unless it goes somewhere you'd start from. Don't put a shadow on it at rest.

  3. Destructive

    The words carry it, never a colour. Ask first, or do it and offer Undo. A destructive button is never the primary.

  4. Installed PWA

    Touch has no hover, so the pressed look (fill and inner ring) is the feedback; the shell's touchstart listener turns on :active in iOS Safari. touch-action: manipulation removes the double-tap wait.

  5. Mac and SwiftUI

    System chrome (toolbar, alerts, sheets) keeps system buttons; ours are for content. In SwiftUI, one custom ButtonStyle reads configuration.isPressed, hover and focus, and draws the same capsule.
