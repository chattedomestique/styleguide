Menu

A short list of actions behind one button. The browser's own popover opens it, closes it on Escape or a tap outside, and gives focus back. No script.

01

Anatomy

A button, and a framed box of quiet buttons that opens under it. Shown here in place, as an open one looks.

1
2
4
  1. 1
    Button.btn popovertarget

    Button, icon-only with ic--more and a name that says whose actions ("More for Shapes"), or with words ("Sort"). It stays down while its menu is open. The browser tells screen readers it's expanded.

  2. 2
    Surface.menu popover

    A container, so square: a 2px frame on paper, 8px inside, and no shadow, since a shadow means "press this". Under the button, end edges aligned; above it when there's no room.

  3. 3
    Item.menu__item

    Button, quiet and full width, words at the start. It closes the menu itself (popovertargetaction="hide"); the app's click does the rest. One to three words, a verb first.

  4. 4
    Rule.menu__rule

    An <hr>, 1px, the menu's whole width: it keeps the destructive item apart.

  5. 5
    Destructive item

    Last, after the rule. The words carry it, never a colour: "Delete deck…". The ellipsis promises a question before anything goes.

02

Variants

One menu, two kinds of button. Both are live: open them with a click, Enter or Space, and the menu fades in at its button; Tab moves through the items; Escape closes and puts you back on the button at once, as it fades.

2.1

More

A thing's other actions: a deck's Rename, Duplicate, Export, Delete.

.btn--icon ic--more .menu .menu__item .menu__rule

The button names the menu's id in popovertarget, and the menu comes right after it in the markup: Tab goes from the button into the menu and on. Four items or so; past seven, it's a screen.

2.2

Sort

A choice that changes how a list is ordered, behind a button with words.

.btn .btn[aria-pressed] .menu__item

The items are Button's toggles: the current order is aria-pressed="true" and carries the check, the others keep its empty slot so the words line up. The app moves the check. Nothing destructive, so no rule.

03

States

The button's and the items' states are Button's. The menu itself has two: closed and open, and where it opens. Shown in place here.

Closed. Just the button, quiet. Hover and focus are Button's: a frame, a lift, the ring.

Open. The button stays down; the menu hangs under it, end edges aligned, 4px off.

Item, hover. Button's quiet hover: the pill fills and lifts. Pointers only.

Item, focus. The hover look and the 3px ring, as on any button. Tab and Shift-Tab move it.

Item, pressed. Down and filled, with the inner ring. Then the menu closes.

Current choice. A check and Button's selected ring, never a fill: a fill is a press.

Where it opens: under the button, end edges aligned; over it, or the other way along, when there's no room; at very large text sizes, centred over the button at the screen's height. Where CSS anchor positioning isn't supported it is a sheet across the bottom of the screen. The .is-* classes force a state for this page and for tests; they are not API.

04

Usage

  1. Do

    Keep it short, verbs first, and put a destructive item last, after the rule, in words that say what goes: "Delete deck…", then ask or offer Undo.

  2. Don't

    Don't hide the only way to do something in a menu or a swipe; don't nest menus; don't give the menu a shadow or round corners. It isn't an ARIA menu, so don't add role="menu" without the arrow keys that promise.

  3. Installed PWA

    Anchored to its button on a phone too; a sheet at the bottom where anchoring isn't there. Light dismiss works with a tap anywhere outside, and Escape on a keyboard.

  4. Mac and SwiftUI

    Menus are system chrome: SwiftUI's Menu, a context menu, and every item in the menu bar with its shortcut. This element is the phone's and the web's.
