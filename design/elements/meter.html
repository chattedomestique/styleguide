Meters

How far along, and “working on it” when nobody knows how long. A bar never stands alone: it always comes with a name and the amount in words.

01

Variants

Three bars, one look: a square 2px frame filled from the start edge, the name on the left, the amount on the right. Which one is a question of meaning, not looks.

1.1

Meter

A value in a range: how much of a deck you know.

.meter .meter__bar role="meter"

The bar is labelled by the name and speaks its aria-valuetext, the same words you see. So the visible amount is aria-hidden and is heard once. --value sets the fill. It replaced the wireframe kit's stand-in.

1.3

Segments

A short deck, one box per card.

.meter--segments .meter__seg

Up to 20 cards; past that, use the meter. The boxes fill from aria-valuenow, so the number a screen reader hears is the number you see. One empty .meter__seg per card.

03

Usage

  1. Do

    Name what's measured and say the amount the way a person would: "5 of 12 known", not "42%". If you can count it, show progress, not a spinner.

  2. Don't

    Don't show a bar without its words, a spinner without its words, or a meter where a number alone would do. Don't use segments past 20 cards.

  3. Installed PWA

    Offline, nothing loads from the network: say what's on the device instead of spinning. A long import keeps its progress on screen, so a person can leave and come back.

  4. Mac and SwiftUI

    ProgressView(value:total:) with a custom style for progress, Gauge for a meter. In system chrome the system spinner stays; in content, this ring, always with a Text.
