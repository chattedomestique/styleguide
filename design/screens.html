What exists

It's a design system, built one element at a time. The foundations are shared by everything; Card was the first element through the gates. These are the ones these screens need; all 27 are listed on the hub, in the order they're being built.

  1. Foundations: built

    Type, space, line, shape, hit targets, colour roles and tones, the gate. Every element reads these.

  2. Card: gates 0–4

    Eight variants, six states. The decks, the stats and the flashcard.

  3. Button: gates 0–4

    The answer buttons on the study screen.

  4. Tag: gates 0–4

    The statuses on the Mac's recent rows.

  5. Meter: gates 0–4

    Progress on the Carry on and Known cards.

  6. Field: gates 0–4

    Not on these screens yet. On the Mac, search is the system's own field.

  7. Frame: gates 0–4

    Every screen here: the rows, the safe areas, the one region that scrolls, and the measured --chrome the study card fits under.

  8. Top bar: gates 0–4

    The study screen's way back, title and Undo.

  9. Tab bar: gates 0–4

    Today's places, with a badge on Decks.

  10. Sidebar: gates 0–4

    Not on these screens: on the Mac the toolbar and sidebar are system chrome. Sidebar is the web shell's, for a tablet or a browser.

01

Phone, installed

A PWA on the home screen: no browser bars, so the app draws its own, with Frame, Top bar and Tab bar. 390 by 844 points, with the safe areas of a phone with a notch. Scroll inside the screen. On a narrow window the frame narrows with it, so it shows a smaller phone than that. The working starter is the installable app: run node design/tools/serve.mjs.

02

Mac window

The same app on a Mac, in a web shell or SwiftUI. The toolbar, sidebar and search field belong to macOS, in its font and colours. Only the content is ours: a frame with no bars, just the region that scrolls. Under 700 points wide the sidebar folds away; a narrow browser window shows that, not a real Mac.

03

What the screens found

Building real screens is the test the specimen page can't run. Wave 3 rebuilt these on Frame, Top bar and Tab bar: five findings are fixed or decided, and the rebuild found two more. The open ones belong in an element's brief.

  1. Fixed: Frame was the biggest gap.

    Every screen needed the same rows, safe areas and scroll region, and wrote them again (.app). Frame is them now, and screens.css is down to the screens' own groups. A root screen keeps a band the height of the status bar that doesn't scroll, so Today's heading never slides under the clock.

  2. Fixed: the study card's --chrome was a guess.

    It was 10rem plus the safe areas. Frame now measures it: 277px on the study screen at 100% text (top bar 109, action row 96, the stage's padding 72), 515px at 150%, and the card is the room that's left.

  3. Fixed: --gutter followed the window, not the pane.

    It was sized by vw. Frame re-points it to its own width (cqi), so a frame that is one pane of a split, like the Mac's content, gets a pane's gutter.

  4. Decided: the tab bar wraps at 200%.

    The labels stay whole and readable, and an item that doesn't fit takes a second row: the bar grows taller, never wider. We don't copy iOS's one-row bar, because its long-press preview is the system's and the web has none.

  5. Decided: on a Mac, the bars aren't ours.

    Toolbar, sidebar and search are system chrome, so Top bar and Tab bar are phone and web elements, and Sidebar is the web shell's. Field's first real use is on the phone.

  6. Open: Tab scrolls the link, not the card.

    A link card wears its focus ring round the whole card, but Tab scrolls the link in its title into view, and Chrome centres it. At 100% no ring here is cut; at 200% a deck tile is taller than half the room, so its top edge is. Frame's scroll padding can't reach that: the card's link would have to be the box that's scrolled to.

  7. Open: at 200% text the bars take most of a phone.

    Both bars grow with the text, as they must to be read: on Study they take 514 of 840px (658 with the scroll region's padding) and the card scrolls. Card now has an 8rem floor, so it stays wide enough for its own answer. Landscape and small phones are wave 5's.

  8. Open: phones want two decks across.

    The grid's 16rem default gives one column on a phone, and a square card one column wide is a poster. Both phone grids set 8rem, and the Mac's 11rem once the frame is wide. If every app does that, the grid needs a compact size.

  9. Fixed: the answer broke mid-word.

    At 200% text the study card set "Hexagon" as "Hexag / on". The answer now has no rem floor: its size follows the card's width, not the text size. A word that still can't fit breaks at a syllable, with hyphens: auto.

  10. Fixed: headings past h4 kept their margins.

    The reset stopped at h4. These screens sit inside a page, so their deck titles are h5, and an app with nested sections gets there too. base.css now resets h5 and h6.
