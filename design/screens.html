What exists

It's a design system, built one element at a time. The foundations are shared by everything; Card is the first element through the gates. These are the ones these screens need; all 27 are listed on the hub, in the order they're being built.

  1. Foundations: built

    Type, space, line, shape, hit targets, colour roles and tones, five icons, the gate. Every element reads these.

  2. Card: gates 0–2

    Eight variants, six states. Inside the screens, every black box is a card.

  3. Button: next

    The answer buttons on the study screen. Also the card's action slot.

  4. Tag: not started

    Statuses on the Mac's recent rows. Drawn with the kit's stand-in today.

  5. Meter: not started

    Progress on the Carry on and Known cards. Also a stand-in today.

  6. Field: not started

    Not on these screens. On the Mac, search is the system's own field.

  7. Nav bar: not started

    The phone's top bar and tab bar. On the Mac the toolbar and sidebar are system chrome.

  8. Frame: not started

    Rows, safe areas and scrolling. Today it's the .app block in screens.css, and every screen needs it.

Phone, installed

A PWA on the home screen: no browser bars, so the app draws its own. 390 by 844 points, with the safe areas of a phone with a notch. Scroll inside the screen. On a narrow window the frame narrows with it, so it shows a smaller phone than that.

Mac window

The same app on a Mac, in a web shell or SwiftUI. The toolbar, sidebar and search field belong to macOS, in its font and colours. Only the content is ours. Under 700 points wide the sidebar folds away; a narrow browser window shows that, not a real Mac.

What the screens found

Building real screens is the test the specimen page can't run. Two of these were bugs and are fixed. The rest are gaps that belong in an element's brief.

  1. Frame is the biggest gap.

    Every screen needed the same rows, safe areas and scroll region (.app in screens.css). A root screen with no top bar must still keep clear of the status bar, or content scrolls under the clock. And Tab scrolls a card's link into view, not the card that wears the focus ring, so a tall tile near the bottom can lose the bottom of its ring. Scroll padding only softens that.

  2. The study card's --chrome is a guess.

    The card needs the bars' height to fit under them, and the app had to estimate it. Frame should publish its bars' real height.

  3. Phones want two decks across.

    The grid's 16rem default gives one column on a phone, and a square card one column wide is a poster. Both phone grids set 8rem. If every app does that, the grid needs a compact size.

  4. The tab bar has no icons, and wraps at 200%.

    Icons v0 has five glyphs and none of them means Today, Decks or Settings. At 200% text the three labels wrap to two rows. Nav bar's brief has to cover both.

  5. On a Mac, the bars aren't ours.

    Toolbar, sidebar and search are system chrome, so Nav bar is a phone element, and Field's first real use is on the phone.

  6. --gutter follows the window, not the pane.

    It's sized by vw: right for a phone or a whole window, too wide for one pane of a split view. The frames here re-point it to the screen; Frame should do the same for panes.

  7. Fixed: the answer broke mid-word.

    At 200% text the study card set "Hexagon" as "Hexag / on". The answer now has no rem floor: its size follows the card's width, not the text size. A word that still can't fit breaks at a syllable, with hyphens: auto.

  8. Fixed: headings past h4 kept their margins.

    The reset stopped at h4. These screens sit inside a page, so their deck titles are h5, and an app with nested sections gets there too. base.css now resets h5 and h6.
