01

Phone, installed

A PWA on the home screen: no browser bars, so the app draws its own, with Frame, Top bar and Tab bar. 390 by 844 points, with the safe areas of a phone with a notch. Scroll inside the screen. On a narrow window the frame narrows with it, so it shows a smaller phone than that. The working starter is the installable app: run node design/tools/serve.mjs.

02

Mac window

The same app on a Mac, in a web shell or SwiftUI. The toolbar, sidebar and search field belong to macOS, in its font and colours. Only the content is ours: a frame with no bars, just the region that scrolls. Under 700 points wide the sidebar folds away; a narrow browser window shows that, not a real Mac.

03

What the screens decided

Real screens test what a specimen page can't. Each of these is now how the system works, and the last is a limit an app should know about.

  1. Frame is the shell.

    Every screen needs the same rows, safe areas and scroll region, so Frame is them, and screens.css holds only the screens' own groups. A root screen keeps a band the height of the status bar that doesn't scroll, so Today's heading never slides under the clock.

  2. The study card fits the room the bars leave.

    Frame measures --chrome: 277px on the study screen at 100% text (top bar 109, action row 96, the stage's padding 72), 515px at 150%, and the card is the room that's left.

  3. --gutter follows the pane, not the window.

    Frame points it at its own width (cqi), so a frame that is one pane of a split, like the Mac's content, gets a pane's gutter.

  4. The tab bar wraps at 200%.

    The labels stay whole and readable, and an item that doesn't fit takes a second row: the bar grows taller, never wider. We don't copy iOS's one-row bar, because its long-press preview is the system's and the web has none.

  5. On a Mac, the bars aren't ours.

    Toolbar, sidebar and search are system chrome, so Top bar and Tab bar are phone and web elements, and Sidebar is the web shell's.

  6. At 200% text the bars take most of a phone.

    Both bars grow with the text, as they must to be read: on Study they take 514 of 840px (658 with the scroll region's padding), and the card scrolls. Card's 8rem floor keeps it wide enough for its own answer.

  7. Phones show two decks across.

    The grid's 16rem default gives one column on a phone, and a square card one column wide is a poster. Both phone grids set --card-min: 8rem, and the Mac's 11rem once the frame is wide.

  8. The answer never breaks mid-word.

    Its size follows the card's width, not the text size, so at 200% "Hexagon" stays whole. A word that still can't fit breaks at a syllable, with hyphens: auto.

  9. Every heading level resets.

    These screens sit inside a page, so their deck titles are h5, and an app with nested sections gets there too. base.css resets h1 to h6.

  10. A limit: Tab scrolls the link, not the card.

    A link card wears its focus ring round the whole card, but Tab scrolls the link in its title into view, and Chrome centres it. At 100% no ring here is cut; at 200% a deck tile is taller than half the room, so its top edge is. Frame's scroll padding can't reach that: the card's link would have to be the box that's scrolled to.
